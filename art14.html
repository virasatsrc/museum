HARI SINGH NALWA

Hari Singh Nalwa (1791-1837) was the most celebrated General of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's cavalry. He joined the Lahore Service, as a personal attendant of the Maharaja, and rose to be a great nobleman, and one of the highly renowned Generals of Indian military history. He is famous for his role in the conquests of Kasur, Sialkot, Attock, Multan, Kashmir, Peshawar and Jamrud.

Hari Singh Nalwa was the Maharaja's most able and trusted General, who expanded the frontier of Sikh Empire to beyond the Indus River right up to the mouth of the Khyber Pass. His other notable achievements include serving as Governor of Kashmir, Peshawar and Hazara, where he established mints on behalf of the Sikh Empire, to facilitate revenue collection.

In 1834, Hari Singh Nalwa annexed Peshawar and built a fort at Jamrud, which dominated the entrance to the Khyber Pass. The Afghans were furious at the audacity of the Sikhs to build a fort in this contested area - in 1836, they mounted an attack upon the Sikh garrison, which continued for several months. The Sikhs eventually emerged victorious, but at a terrible cost - the death of Hari Singh Nalwa. Hari Singh Nalwa had thus, not only defended Jamrud and Peshawar, but prevented the Afghans from ravaging the entire North-West Frontier. Following his death, no further conquests were made in this direction - the Khyber Pass continued as the Sikh frontier till the annexation of Punjab by the British.
