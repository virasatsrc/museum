2 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 2 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

2 Sikh was raised on 1 August 1846 at Ludhiana as the "Regiment of Loodhiana." Before Independence, the battalion participated in both World Wars. Between 1860 and 1878, it fought gallantly at Gazni and Ahmed Khel. In the fiercely fought Battle of Tofrek in 1885, the battalion distinguished itself and was awarded the Battle Honour "Tofrek."

During the First World War, the battalion fought at Neuve Chapelle, Ypres, La-Bassee, St Julien and Festubert. It won the second Victoria Cross of the unit, the first having been awarded to Maj Petergill in 1857. In recognition of its spectacular performance since raising and its distinguished service during the Quetta earthquake of 1935, the battalion was granted the title "Royal." As part of 4 Infantry Division, it later fought in Italy and Greece during the Second World War.

In 1948, during Operation Polo, the battalion earned the distinction of receiving the first Ashoka Chakra of Independent India, awarded posthumously to Hav Bachittar Singh.

During 1956-57, Hav Joginder Singh was awarded the Ashoka Chakra posthumously for his gallantry in counter-insurgency operations in Nagaland.

In 1962, the battalion represented India in Gaza as part of UNEF. During the Indo-Pak War of 1965, it captured the formidable Pakistani post "Raja" in the Poonch Sector. In the 1971 Indo-Pak War, at very short notice, the battalion captured Pulkanjri Post.

During its tenures in Jammu & Kashmir from 1991-94 and 1998-2001, the battalion earned two COAS Unit Citations and one Army Commander's Unit Citation.

From 2012 to 2013, the battalion was deployed in South Sudan with UNMISS, where it was awarded the Force Commander's Commendation.
