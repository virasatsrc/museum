Sporting Prowess of the Sikhs

Title: Sporting Prowess of the Sikhs
Category: Beyond Battle Fd
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical

The SIKH Regiment has not only produced brave soldiers for the battlefield, but also outstanding sportsmen of repute, who have conquered the playing fields in various sports. The regiment took immense pains to discover, groom and train sports talent. From this 'spirit of excellence', emerged outstanding sportsmen who made the regiment, the army and the nation proud.

One stellar modern day example of this is the legendary Lieutenant Colonel Haripal Kaushik, VrC, who was awarded the Vir Chakra for his exemplary leadership and bravery in the Indo-China War of 1962, and later was conferred with the Arjuna Award for his extraordinary contribution towards the game of Hockey, which included two Olympic gold medals.

Sports for the Khalsas has been as old as the religion itself, with the second guru, Guru Angad Dev Ji holding Wrestling matches to promote good health in Tarn Taran. Guru Gobind Singh played 'Khido Kundi', an ancient type of Field Hockey, as a child, and excelled in Archery, Swordsmanship and Horse Riding.

Tug of War was very popular in the British Indian Army. In February 1919, XLVII SIKHS athletes won many laurels at the Central Sports Meet in Cairo, and won the Challenge Cup for Indian Troops at the Egyptian Expeditionary Force Sports Championship in Alexandria. The Regiment has since produced 12 Olympians. Quoit Throwing was another sport where the Sikhs had no match, able to cut a stick in two at 50 yards.

Hockey was first introduced in the Regiment in 1900. In 1907, the Loodianah Sikhs won the Punjab Native Army Hockey Tournament. The Sikh Regimental Centre Hockey team was raised at Ambala in 1948, producing players like Brigadier HJS Chimni and Lieutenant Colonel Balbir Singh representing India. Other international sports stars include Subedar Jit Singh (Wrestling), Subedar Prakash Singh (Athletics) and Subedar Kaur Singh (Boxing). Subedar Kaur Singh, VSM, is the only Indian boxer to have fought Muhammad Ali in the ring.

In Mountaineering, CHM Gurcharan Singh of 5 SIKH skied down Mount Trishul, creating a world record. Lieutenant Colonel JC Joshi (Padma Shri and Arjuna Award) of 9 SIKH was a member of the first successful Indian Everest expedition in May 1965. Recently, Subedar Manpreet Singh (Boxing), Subedar Ranjit Singh (Rowing) won medals at the 2010 Asian Games. Havildar Nittin (Handball), Havildar Sukhminder Singh (Shot Put), Havildar Bharat Bhushan (Karate) and Havildar Jaswinder Singh (Rowing) have continued this proud sporting legacy in international arenas.
