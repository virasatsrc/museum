Title: HH Maharaja Yadavindra Singh
Category: Regimental Icons
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical

1. Maharaja Yadavindra Singh, the elder son of Maharaja Bhupinder Singh, GCSI, GCIE, GCVO, GBE, of the Princely State of Patiala (1900-1938) is a leading icon of the SIKH Regiment. He became the Maharaja on 23 March 1938 and was the ninth and last ruling Maharaja of Patiala (1938-1971). He was also a diplomat, sports administrator and cricketer. Born at Patiala in 1914, Maharaja Yadavindra attended Aitchison College in Lahore. He served in the Patiala State Police, became its Inspector General and served in Malaya, Italy and Burma during the second world war.

2. Following his accession to the throne of Patiala, Yadavindra pursued a political and diplomatic career, serving as chancellor of the Chamber of Princes from 1943 to 1944.

3. In 1947, when India gained independence, he was the pro-chancellor of the Chamber of Princes and played a major role along with Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, in the accession of Princely States into India. Yadavindra later served as President of the Indian Olympic Association, Chairman of Indian Horticulture Development Council, President of the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) and led several Indian delegations to the United Nations General Assembly, UNESCO and FAO. He also served as Indian Ambassador to Italy (1965-1966) and as Indian Ambassador to the Netherlands from 1971 until 17 June 1974, when he died suddenly in office at The Hague from heart failure, aged 60. On specific instructions of then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, he was cremated with full state honours.

4. His lasting legacy includes the Yadavindra Public School located at Lal Bagh Palace, which was donated by Maharaja Yadavindra Singh. The Yadavindra Public School, Mohali is also named after him and was established by the members of his family. In 1956, he also donated the Anand Bhawan, a 150 bigha palace, to the government for setting up a holiday home for poor children.

5. Maharaja Yadavindra Singh was appointed as the Honorary Colonel of The SIKH Regiment on 17 July 1954, thus continuing the third-generation association of the rulers of Patiala with the Regiment. The Maharaja was instrumental in laying out the Centre at Meerut, particularly the War Memorial and the Gurudwara Sahib, in the august presence of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, the Prime Minister. His ever-willing guidance was the pillar of strength which assisted the historic Colour Presentation Parade on 15 April 1968 which was reviewed by Dr Zakir Husain, President of India.
