GURU TEGH BAHADUR JI

Guru Tegh Bahadur was born at Amritsar on 01 April 1621 to Guru Hargobind and Mata Nanaki. As a teenager, he had earned his spurs in the Battle of Kartarpur in 1635; so when the Guru's mantle was placed on him in 1665, he was ready to preside over a momentous period of Sikh history. After establishing the settlement of Anandpur Sahib, he conducted tours outside Punjab to spread Sikh ideas, alongside establishing secular traditions through community water wells and langars. He also exhorted people to stand firm against tyranny. During this time, he settled his family at Patna for sometime, where his son Gobind Rai was born. His rising popularity among the masses was a direct challenge to political authority of Mughal Emperor, Aurangzeb, who summoned the Guru to Delhi. He was sentenced to death and beheaded on 11 November 1675. Before his body could be quartered and exposed to public view, Bhai Jaita, a devotee quietly took it under cover of darkness and respectfully had it cremated a few miles away. The sites of the execution and subsequent cremation in Delhi are marked by Gurudwara Sis Ganj and Gurudwara Rakab Ganj, respectively. The place where his severed head was cremated in Anandpur is also called Sis Ganj ('the place of the head').

'The truly enlightened ones are those who neither incite fear in others, nor fear anyone themselves'
Guru Tegh Bahadur
