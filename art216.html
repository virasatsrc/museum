From War Drums to Military Bands

Title: From War Drums to Military Bands
Category: From War Drums to Military Bands
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
Sikh armies traditionally advanced to the sound of nigaras, or kettledrums, and nar-singhas, or horn pipes. Maharaja Ranjit Singh expanded this martial custom by organising drummers and buglers in the Khalsa Army. Under the regimental system, bands accompanied active battalions on field service as stretcher-bearers, linking music with military duty.

The first major development came in 1862, when Colonel C.C.G. Ross sent twenty-one men of XIV Ferozepore Sikhs to Calcutta for training on brass instruments. By 1888, the battalion possessed a thirty-four-member brass band led by Havildar Daler Singh. Colonel G.N. Channer, Victoria Cross, later raised an eighteen-member Pipe Band with assistance from Northamptonshire pipers who had accompanied the Viceroy to India in 1885. XV Ludhiana Sikhs received twenty drummers on its raising in 1846. XLV Rattray's Sikhs formed its band under the General Order of 1877, while the Pipes and Drums of XXXVI Sikhs were raised with the battalion at Jalandhar on 23 March 1887.
