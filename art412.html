Title: Battle of OP Hill (1965)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

The Battle of OP Hill (located on a hill feature called 'Chuh-i-Nar') took place after the cease fire came into effect on 23 September in the Indo-Pakistan War, 1965. OP Hill was an important Observation Post (OP) of Pakistani Army in Balnoi Sector, 20 km South-West of Poonch, from where the Pakistani Artillery brought direct artillery fire on the Mendhar-Poonch road axis. The tactical feature was also a foothold for the adversary to launch future operations towards Mendhar - it was thus, essential to capture OP Hill. Following a failed battalion-level offensive on 06-07 October 1965, a full-fledged brigade attack under 120 Infantry Brigade was mounted on 02 November 1965 - 7 SIKH, 2 DOGRA, 5 SIKH LIGHT INFANTRY, 169 Mountain Regiment and 23 Mountain Composite Regiment were part of this attack.

The plan involved an enveloping attack from three directions, combined with company strength stops in the rear, to prevent reinforcements and ambush the withdrawing enemy. 7 SIKH attack was launched in three phases, going almost frontal. In the First Phase, Alpha Company was to capture Jungle Hill. In Second Phase, Bravo Company was to take OP Hill and Charlie Company was held as Reserve.

At 0310 hrs on 03 November 1965, with just two hours of darkness left, the attack was launched on Jungle Hill. This was led by Alpha Company, under Captain Sansar Singh, and was followed by Major Sharma's Bravo Company. The climb was extremely steep, and adding to the difficulty was blistering enemy artillery shelling, with Machine Guns and other support weapons firing from the adjoining features, which were still held by the enemy. The advance slowed, men dispersed and casualties mounted. Yet the companies continued to move steadily forward, all the time being exhorted and encouraged by the Commanding Officer, Lieutenant Colonel Bhagat Singh. To take pressure off from the frontal attack, Charlie Company, under Major (later Major General) Trilochan Singh approached its objective, Long Tree from both ends of a precipitous rock face. The going was tough, and the company had to literally clamber up on hands and feet. On reaching the top, besides the very intense enemy fire, they were confronted with heavy density of Anti-Personnel Mines and Booby Traps.

Eventually having gained adequate ground, the Sikhs made a bayonet charge, and fierce hand-to-hand fighting ensued. The enemy continued to put up strong resistance, but could not check the determined offensive. Each enemy bunker had to be fiercely fought for and destroyed. Wave after wave, the assaulting troops overcame all resistance, capturing lines of trenches, one after another. Soon, OP Hill was captured by 7 SIKH, thus ensuring victory in this battle. Outstanding leadership was displayed at all levels. Major (later Major General) Trilochan Singh, Captain (later Colonel) Sansar Singh, Lieutenant (later Colonel) Hardial Singh, four JCOs and a number of NCOs were severely wounded.

The battalion losses were not substantial, considering the ferocity of fighting. 21 men were killed, mostly from Subedar Bikkar Singh's leading platoon, and 50 were wounded. By contrast, the enemy suffered very heavy casualties. 7 SIKH was awarded Battle Honour - 'OP Hill' and Theatre Honour - 'Jammu and Kashmir 1965' for its gallant actions in the 1965 Indo-Pakistan War.
