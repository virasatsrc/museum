GURU ANGAD DEV JI

Guru Angad Dev Ji was born on 31 March 1504 at Sarai Naga, Muktsar in present day Faridkot district to Pheru Mal and Mata Ramo. His childhood name was Lehna. Before dying, Guru Nanak expressed his preference for Lehna: 'thou art Angad, a part of my body', as the second Guru, over his own sons. On the advice of Guru Nanak, Angad left Kartarpur and 'lit the Guru's lamp' in Khadoor, near Tarn Taran. He was the Guru for 13 years (1539-1552) and in his own quiet way, ensured that the Guru's teachings were propagated further and the fledgling faith grows with discipline. He formalised 'Gurmukhi' as a language by taking 35 letters of the acrostic composed by Guru Nanak and selecting appropriate letters from other scripts prevalent in North India. Guru Angad also had two sons, but he chose Guru Amar Das, his 73-year old disciple, as his successor. The second Guru died on 29 March 1552 at Khadoor. The Khadoor Sahib Gurudwara which stands there today bears testimony to Guru Angad's contribution to the Sikh faith.
