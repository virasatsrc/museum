Honours and Indianisation

Title: Honours and Indianisation
Category: Honours and Indianisation
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
In 1935, 2/11 Sikh rendered outstanding rescue and security service after the devastating Quetta earthquake, despite suffering casualties of its own. The Viceroy, the Earl of Willingdon, presented the battalion a Certificate of Appreciation. On 1 June 1935, in recognition of its distinguished service since 1846, the title "Royal" was conferred upon it.

The period also marked the gradual Indianisation of the officer corps. Lieutenant Thakur Singh, MC, and Lieutenant Mit Singh, MC, IDSM and Bar, both of 5/11 Sikh, were among the first five Indian JCOs granted the King's Commission after the Great War. From December 1934, 5/11 Sikh became the Regiment's selected battalion for receiving Indian Military Academy graduates. The first were Second Lieutenants Diwan Ranjit Rai and Mirza Hamid Hussain, followed between 1935 and 1940 by officers including Harbaksh Singh, Bhag Singh, M.S. Dhillon, K.K. Bhandari, Mohinder Singh, P.P. Singh, Y.S. Panwar and Gurmit Singh. These developments created the organisational and leadership foundation with which the Regiment entered the Second World War.
