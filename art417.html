Title: Kargil Operations (1999)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

In1999, 8 SIKH commanded by Colonel SP Singh, had just moved from Pathankot to Pulwama, when it received orders to move to the Drass Sub-Sector on 12 May. The Battalion was inducted in Operation Vijay on 14 May 1999 and tasked to evict a few intruders from the dominating heights in Drass and Kargil. With limited acclimatization and winter clothing, the Battalion was launched into the operation to clear Tiger Hill, Point 4460 and Point 4195, at altitudes ranging from 13,500 feet to 16,000 feet. Rugged terrain, invincible heights, extreme climate and the absence of enemy intelligence and lack of artillery support made the task a formidable one

Alpha Company was tasked to capture Point 4195 and Point 4460. Taking up the challenge up-front, the troops moved straight up the formidable height, braving the steep climbs and descent over glaciated terrain. By 18 May, Point 4460 and Point 4195 were captured, and the Battalion's Tactical Headquarters was established at Point 4460. On the same night of 18 May, Bravo Company, while negotiating the steep, snow covered Southern slopes of Tiger Hill, came under heavy automatic fire from Tiger Hill. This confirmed heavy enemy concentration in the Tiger Hill Complex, to include Tiger Hill Top, Eastern Spur and Western Spur (comprising India Gate, Helmet, Rocky Knob and Rhino Horn). This was contrary to the previous intelligence reports, which indicated the presence of only a few intruders at Tiger Hill Top. Two companies then attempted to clear Tiger Hill from the East and the North, but intense enemy automatic fire stalled any further progress.

Delta Company was then ordered to circumvent the Tiger Hill feature. Accordingly, they occupied Pariyon-Ka-Talab from the North. The initial task for 8 SIKH was to cut off the Western Ridge of Tiger Hill. Later, the Battalion was ordered to isolate Tiger Hill from all directions, and cut off any reinforcement or withdrawal from the top. However, heavy enemy fire including artillery, Machine Guns and rockets, prevented attempts to secure a foothold. Casualties mounted as the Battalion was totally exposed on barren mountain face. A platoon of Bravo Company, led by Subedar Sardar Singh, was tasked to invest Umba La, to prevent enemy infiltration from the South of Drass. They got to Point 4245, and established a block, which remained in position for the next 35 days, i.e., till 30 June. A number of attempts were made to cut off Tiger Hill from the Western side by sub-units of 8 SIKH and other units, including Special Forces Teams, but these were mostly unsuccessful. During these forays, a few casualties were also suffered.

On the night of 03/04 July, 18 GRENADIERS was tasked to capture Tiger Hill, and 8 SIKH to provide a firm base, simulate attacks from the Southern and Northern directions, and provide one company strength as reserve to deal with any contingency. 18 GRENADIERS launched their attack from the Eastern and South-Eastern approaches. After the Eastern Spur was cleared, further advance towards Tiger Hill Top was effectively interfered with enemy fire on 04 July. 8 SIKH was tasked to gain a foothold on the Western Spur of Tiger Hill, with a view to cut it off and provide a firm base for its subsequent capture. The Western Spur, comprising of features Rhino Horn, Rocky Knob, Helmet and India Gate, was strongly held by the enemy, and was totally dominated by Tiger Hill Top. The approach to establish the firm base was extremely steep, with a gradient of 60-80 degrees. It was not only a very challenging objective to capture, but extremely difficult to hold, due to the limited space available and enemy domination.

The plan was to capture India Gate and Helmet from the Western approach, with surprise being the vital ingredient. Accordingly, on the night of 04/05 July, an ad-hoc company of two officers, led by Major Ravindra Singh, four JCOs and 52 OR, traversed the difficult terrain, amidst enemy shelling and automatic fire, to the base of the objective. Extremely bad weather, fog and rain came as a boon. The company negotiated the steep slopes undetected, with high standards of mountaineering skills. Three different teams probed towards the objective. The enemy was totally surprised with the sudden assault of these teams on India Gate and Helmet and opened up with automatic weapons. But the brave Khalsas retaliated with Rocket Launcher fire, and forced the enemy to flee. India Gate and helmet were captured at 0400 hrs on 05 July and Tiger Hill Top was completely cut off from the Western Spur.

The enemy was desperate to keep the Western route of maintenance open. At first light, the enemy defences on Tiger Hill opened up in full fury, with the artillery firing air-bursts, and multiple Air Defence Guns firing from two directions. This firing continued unabated for 24 hours. Own troops endured not only the withering fire and bombardment, but the freezing temperatures, without food or medical aid. It was followed by several counter-attacks, including one led by two Pakistani officers, who nearly reached the inner defence perimeter of India Gate. 8 SIKH was resolute in defending its territory - Subedar Nirmal Singh who was severely wounded, did a bayonet charge on the advancing enemy and halted the attack. Both Pakistani officers were killed in this action - one was later identified as Captain Karnal Sher Khan of 12 NLI, who was awarded Pakistan's highest gallantry award 'Nishan-e-Haider', based on the account of his bravery penned by 8 SIKH. Subedar Nirmal Singh and Naib Subedar Karnail Singh made the supreme sacrifice in this action.

The gallant Sikhs held on to Western Spur, and repulsed the two counter attacks by 0630 hrs, inflicting heavy casualties on the enemy. The enemy suffered more than 30 killed, including three officers. They fled, leaving behind the bodies of their soldiers, including officers. Major Ravindra Singh, though wounded, held on to the Western Spur for the rest of the day. Reinforcement from Alpha Company arrived at night and some of the wounded were evacuated. 18 GRENADIERS then resumed the assault and occupied Tiger Hill Complex by 0800 hrs on 08 July. For display of resilience of a high order and marked gallantry, Subedar Nirmal Singh, Naib Subedar Karnail Singh and Sepoy Satpal Singh were awarded the Vir Chakra. The Battalion was awarded Battle Honour - 'Tiger Hill', Theatre Honour-Kargil, COAS Unit Citation and a Special Citation by Punjab Government. In addition to three Vir Chakras, it also earned 11 Sena Medals and 12 Mention-in-Dispatches.

14 SIKH also participated with elan and gave an excellent account of itself at Chorbat La during the Kargil War in 1999. It was airlifted from Chandigarh to Leh and tasked to plug the gap between Turtuk and Yaldor sectors which were lightly held earlier. The Battalion achieved complete moral ascendancy over the enemy and won One Vir Chakra, three Sena Medals, two Mention-in-Dispatches and a number of other awards.
