Partition, Ambala and Meerut

Title: Partition, Ambala and Meerut
Category: Partition, Ambala and Meerut
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
Partition forced the Centre to abandon much of its infrastructure, including its Gurdwara Sahib and Saragarhi memorials. Lieutenant Colonels R. Bamfield and D.O.T. Dykes, assisted by Subedar Major and Honorary Lieutenant Badan Singh and Honorary Captain Tajinder Singh, nevertheless secured the movement of Colours, mess silver, weapons and equipment. The Centre reached Allenby Lines, Ambala, in September 1947. Lieutenant Colonel Dykes and his wife were killed by raiders at Baramulla on 27 October, while Colonel M.M. Badshah became the first Indian Commandant and established the Centre at its new station.

The Kashmir war immediately required trained reinforcements for 1, 2 and 7 Sikh and for the newly raised 16, 17 and 18 Sikh. The Centre also sheltered soldiers' families uprooted by Partition and cared for nearly 250 Kashmiri boys, many orphaned around Baramulla. This institution later developed into Bal Bhavan. Between 11 and 15 March 1952, the Centre moved in four military specials to Meerut. During its twenty-four years there, it expanded steadily, particularly after the 1962 war, and created major regimental institutions, including the Nand Singh Stadium, inaugurated on 26 December 1955, the War Memorial, Gurdwara Sahib and Sikh Museum.
