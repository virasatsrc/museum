MAJOR GENERAL VR KHANOLKAR (December 1949 – April 1953)

Major General Vikram Ramji Khanolkar was born in Quetta (now in Pakistan) on 28 June 1905. He received his early education at Quetta before further studies at Wilson College, Bombay. In 1927, he was enlisted as a Sepoy in the 1st Battalion of the 4th Bombay Grenadiers and received a Viceroy's Commission the following year. In 1929, he was selected for a King's Commission as a KCIO and passed out from the Royal Military Academy, Sandhurst on 29 January 1931. From 1932 to 1938, he served in various battalions of the SIKH Regiment. During the Second World War, he was posted at the SIKH Regimental Centre, Nowshera and also fought in North Africa and Italy with 5/11 SIKHS. He later commanded 6/11 SIKHS in Waziristan as a Lieutenant Colonel in 1945.

He was one of the first Indian Brigadiers in the Regiment after Independence and was appointed as Commandant of a refugee camp at Kurukshetra. He then briefly commanded the Jalandhar Sub Area and a Brigade in Jammu before being promoted as Major General and taking charge as GOC, Delhi Area on 05 August 1949. In January 1952, Khanolkar assumed command of 20 Infantry Division at Calcutta.

Major General VR Khanolkar took over as Colonel of the SIKH Regiment in December 1949, and remained so till August 1952. He did yeoman service to the Regiment by formulating SOPs and settling down the SRC at Ambala. He died in harness as Colonel Commandant and GOC, 20 Infantry Division due to cardiac arrest on 29 August 1952, while aboard a train travelling from Ranchi to Calcutta.

The highest gallantry award of Independent India, the Param Vir Chakra, has been designed by his wife, Mrs Savitri Khanolkar, who was of Swiss descent.
