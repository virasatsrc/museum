Title: Capture of Richhmar Gali (1948)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

After Uri and Handwara Operations, 1 SIKH was under command of Lieutenant Colonel Kehar Singh, IDSM, as part of 163 Infantry Brigade. The brigade was being commanded by Brigadier Harbakhsh Singh, the previous Commanding Officer of 1 SIKH. The resources were grossly inadequate, but despite that, further operations were planned on both axes, i.e. beyond Uri, towards Domel, and from Trahagam to Tithwal. 1 SIKH led the advance, and regardless of all opposition, secured Chowkibal on 21 May 1948, and two days later, captured Tithwal on 23 May. 1 SIKH was assigned the task of clearing the heights of Richhmar Gali and Point 7229. The approach to Richmar Gali involved a climb up a steep gradient, which could be negotiated only in single file.

On 30 May, assaults were launched against the strongly held ridge and wooded crests, well-covered by series of Machine Gun posts. The CO had declared that even though the enemy was strongly entrenched, the position had to be captured. 1 SIKH assaulted the enemy (identified as the 3/12 FRONTIER FORCE REGIMENT, the erstwhile and venerable 53rd (ROYAL) SIKHS of the undivided Indian Army) and severe hand-to-hand fighting ensued. 1 SIKH ejected the enemy and created very strong defence works on this densely forested feature. On 04 June, under heavy artillery fire, the enemy launched a strong attack, but was held back by Jemadar Harchand Singh's platoon, who tenaciously held on to the captured position. 53 enemy pers were killed and a large number of arms and ammunition were captured. This battle, fought on 04 June, coincided with Gallipoli Day, that marked the outstanding action undertaken by 1 SIKH, 33 years earlier. Thus, there was an added celebration.

However, the re-capture of Tithwal, with Richmar Gali as a vital objective, became the Pakistan Army's obsession. A reinforced brigade, with artillery, was deployed to encircle the position, and their General-in-Command broadcast that the Tithwal Valley - Nastachhun Pass, up to Kupwara, was to be 'an Eid gift for Pakistan'. A four-man section of 1 SIKH on Small Knoll, covering the knife-edged ridge, was to beat back battalion-sized attacks. The man of the moment was Lance Naik Karam Singh, who had earlier won the Military Medal in Burma. From the evening of 12 October 1948, heavy artillery and mortar fire damaged the main defences, including the defence works on Small Knoll.

Throughout 13 October, the battle was fought. Wave after wave of assaulting Pakistan army troops were beaten back. Karam Singh, though wounded, fought tooth-and-nail, bayoneting those who came near his trench, holding Small Knoll for over three hours against five waves of attack. On the night of 13 October and the following morning, the seventh and last of the attacks was beaten off. Although every bunker on the knoll was destroyed, the enemy could not capture even a single one. While 1 SIKH had 15 killed and 263 wounded, the Pakistan Army lost over 300 men and 500 wounded. Many Pakistanis were taken as Prisoners of War.

Major General KS Thimayya, DSO, GOC 19 Infantry Division (later COAS), was brimming with pride and praise when he referred to the Battle of Richhmar Gali as a 'magnificent fight'. Lance Naik Karam Singh won immortal fame and was awarded India's highest gallantry award, the Param Vir Chakra, for his fiercely proud spirit. He was described as a dauntless and born leader of men in crisis, where spirits could neither be subdued by fire nor hardship. 1 SIKH was awarded Battle Honours 'Srinagar 1947' and 'Tithwal 1948' and the Theatre Honour' Jammu and Kashmir
