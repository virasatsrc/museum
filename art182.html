Title: HISTORY OF SIKH REGIMENTAL BAND
Category: Beyond Battle Fd.
Artist: Unknown Artist
Year: Historical

The history of a band in the Sikh Army dates back to the reign of Maharaja Ranjit Singh who trained drummers and buglers to set pace of troops on the parade ground as well as during marches. The first bands of the SIKH Regiment were constituted alongside its raising in 1846, when XIV FEROZEPORE SIKHS at Ferozepore and XV LUDHIANA SIKHS at ludhiana raised their bands, each comprising of 12 members who played traditional instruments like Ganar Pipes, Big Drums, Taras, Dhols and Bugles - the capability of the band then was judged by who could play the instruments the loudest!

In 1862, Colonel CCG Ross, CB, the fifth Commanding Officer of 1 SIKH and a connoisseur of rhythm and music, dispatched a group of 21 Sikh Soldiers to the Bengal Native Army Centre at Calcutta (now Kolkata), to obtain training in Brass Band instruments. By 1888, the Battalion had its own Brass Band comprising of 34 members with Havildar Daler Singh as the first Band Major.

Colonel GN Channer, VC, the seventh Commanding Officer of 1 SIKH, a known musician, undertook the task of raising a Pipes and Drums Band. Under his able guidance and assistance from the Northamptonshire Pipers, who had accompanied the Viceroy to India in 1885, the Battalion raised an 18-member Pipes and Drums Band. The next band to be raised was that of XLV RATTRAY'S vide Government Gazette Order 4 of 1877 with a monthly maintenance allowance of Rupees One Hundred.

Both bands of the SIKH Regimental Centre (SRC) were raised as part of XXXVI SIKH, now 4 SIKH, on 12 April 1887, which later on merged with XXXV SIKH (the present SRC) at Nowshera, now in Pakistan. The Band continued their journey with the SRC, till it finally settled down at Ramgarh in 1976.

In 1905, 3 SIKH Band participated in a tattoo event which was precursor to the Edinburgh Military Tattoo, conducted at the Esplanade of Edinburgh Castle. The band was adjudged the Best Band of Indian Army in a competition held at New Delhi in 1910.

On 21 November 1905, 4 SIKH, along with its Band, took part in a review held at Rawalpindi by HRH The Prince of Wales, and in November, took part in the Coronation Ceremonies of their Imperial Majesties, The King Emperor and Queen Empress at Delhi.

Prior to the First World War, it was a custom with Indian Army Units to have German Bandmasters. This practice of German Bandmasters ended with outbreak of the First World War, and Indian Officers, or VCOs were asked to step in as Band Masters. They adapted themselves well, and showed their proficiency and excellence at each stage, and brought laurels to the Regiment. The history of SIKH Regimental Bands is written in golden letters since the beginning. Subedar Gulab Singh, the Bandmaster of 1 SIKH, was called to Kenya to train the Kenyan Police Band. He became a celebrated personality and led the Indian Band Contingent during the Coronation Ceremony of 1911 in London. In 1946, the Military Band of the SIKH Regiment was selected for a tour of Burma and Malaya.

The Military Band and a major part of the Pipers, accompanied 2 SIKH to Gaza in 1962. Whilst here, the Buglers were invited for Armistice Day at the Jerusalem War Memorial. The Buglers' performance was much appreciated, admired and they were specially complimented for a flawless sounding of the 'Last Post'.

The Pipes and Drums Band of 2 SIKH was also part of United Nation Force at Gaza, where it participated in the Edinburgh Military Tattoo. They were applauded by the British public and media because of their colourful and impressive uniform, glorious war records and outstanding performance.

In 1954, both the Pipers and the Drummers played at the Coronation Ceremony of the King of Bhutan, creating the most melodious atmosphere. Again, on 08 November 2007, the Military Band took the center stage at IMTRAT, Bhutan, to celebrate the birthday of the Fourth Druk Gyalpo, His Majesty Jigme Singye Wangchuck, receiving much adulation.

In 1971, on a special request of the King of Oman, the Band was sent to Oman. Led by Subedar Kashmir Singh, the musical display received accolades from the Omanian Royals. In 2003, two pipers of 2 SIKH, Havildars Jaswinder Singh and Balwinder Singh were specially selected to go to Europe and participate in the Remembrance Ceremony of the martyrs of Second World War.

The Pipes and Drums Band of the SIKH Regiment was adjudged the Best Band during Army Day Parade held on 15 January 2022.

In recent times, units have also raised their own Jazz Bands with modern instruments and acoustic systems. These experienced bands can play Hindi, Punjabi and English music with equal elan. 20 SIKH Jazz Band has had the rare opportunity of performing before the three Service Chiefs in Delhi where they received a standing Ovation from the audience.
