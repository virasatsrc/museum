Title: Battle of Raja Picquet (1965)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

After the Hajipir Pass was captured on 28 August 1965, the focus shifted to capture of Raja and Rani Picquets, which were critical to establishment of the Uri-Poonch Link. On 29 August 1965, 2 SIKH was moved to Poonch after the Battalion's gallant action in Chhamb where it had captured 10 enemy posts within three days. The Raja Picquet on the feature Point 7702 was a key defended locality and important to the enemy's forward defence line. 2 SIKH was tasked to capture Raja Picquet - a challenging task, demanding not only determination, but also tactical ingenuity.

Lieutenant Colonel Narendra Nath Khanna, Commanding Officer, 2 SIKH was confident of capturing Raja Piquet. His plan of attack was to employ Alpha, Bravo and Delta Companies in a simultaneous attack from two directions. The battle commenced on night 5/6 September when Alpha Company reached the Forming Up Place. The enemy seemed to have discovered the forward movement and deployment - there was heavy and concentrated artillery and Machine Gun fire which delayed the assault by an hour. It was already daylight when the assaulting companies approached the objective. The troops got into assaulting formation and moved up, hitting the folds in the ground, and then began crawling up. As Alpha Company approached the objective, men began to fall to enemy fire, the first among them being Naib Subedar Darshan Singh, a former national athlete. Casualties of the company piled on, and troops were pinned down.

The situation of Bravo Company was no different. Its first casualty was the Company Commander, Major KC Kalley, who was seriously wounded. In true spirit, his Second-in-Command, Subedar Balwant Singh, proved his worth - he assumed command and continued the attack, until he himself was killed. The three assaulting companies kept moving up along with the Commanding Officer's party, all under intense enemy fire. The assault was held up at the wire obstacle in front of the picquet. Behind this were the main defences, comprising trenches and pill boxes, with a dense minefield in front. The Commanding Officer ordered his men to move forward and continue the assault. As troops were crossing the wire obstacle, Lieutenant Colonel NN Khanna was hit by the fire of .50 Anti-Aircraft Gun. Yet, he kept inspiring his men to keep attacking- their leader's injury electrified the men and they were all over the enemy trenches.

The three Company Commanders, Captains Jai Singh, Surjit Singh and AS Dharni, also led their men from the front. Acts of gallantry and junior leadership were witnessed all over the objective area. It was Naik Chand Singh (an outstanding javelin thrower), who single-handedly silenced an enemy Machine Gun, which was holding up the assault, as the officers and men fought for every inch and moved from bunker to bunker. Lieutenant Colonel NN Khanna, along with his party was continuously in the open, when he was again hit by Machine Gun fire. He was carried down immediately by the Intelligence Havildar Ujjagar Singh and others to the Regimental Aid Post. He breathed his last while being evacuated to the Advanced Dressing Station - his last question was whether Raja Picquet had been captured successfully.

After many hours of dogged resistance by the enemy, Raja Picquet was captured by 2 SIKH - the troops aptly described the outcome in Puniabi, "Raia litta, Raja ditta" (we captured 'Raja' (Picquet), only to lose our Raja (King)). 2 SIKH was awarded Battle Honour - 'Raja' and Theatre Honour-Jammu and Kashmir 1965' for its operations. Lieutenant Colonel NN Khanna was awarded the Maha Vir Chakra (posthumous). Naik Chand Singh was awarded Vir Chakra, Havildar Baldev Singh Sena Medal (posthumous, and Major (later Colonel) KC Kalley, Lieutenant (later Major General) Keshav Singh, Havildar Dalip Singh and Naik Surjit Singh were Mentioned-in-Dispatches for their gallantry and leadership.

The unit lost 40 brave Sikhs including the Commanding Officer while 104 men were wounded. Among the martyred was the battalion's entire Hockey Team and many other outstanding sportsmen.
