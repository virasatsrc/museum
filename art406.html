Title: Battle of Shalateng (1947)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

The decisive Battle of Shalateng, fought on 07 November 1947, was part of overall operations for the Defence of Kashmir, 1947-48. This battle removed the threat to Srinagar and the Kashmir Valley. On the night of 06 November, Bravo Company, 1 SIKH was attacked by nearly 5000 raiders. But all attacks were beaten back, inflicting heavy casualties on the enemy. The nest day in a brilliant tactical move, while enveloping attacks were carried out by the Kumaonis, and a troop of Armoured Cars of 7 CAVALRY, 1 SIKH launched frontal attacks. Totally astounded by this assault from three sides, the enemy started fleeing desperately along the road to Baramulla, leaving behind their lorries, dumps of ammunition and supplies. The raiders rout was complete, and they fled back in panic, leaving about 500 raiders dead.

An immediate advance was then launched along the main axis, with a company of 1 SIKH closely pursuing the fleeing enemy in civilian buses. 1 SIKH recaptured Pattan the same evening by 1830 hrs, killed another 150 and wounded 200 of the raiders. They also captured vehicles, wireless sets, arms and ammunition. Some of the captured Pathans belonged to the Frontier Force regular troops, apparently on leave, thus, immediately exposing Pakistan's perfidy. On the morning of 08 November, the Battalion moved ahead and flushed out several enemy positions. It entered a devastated Baramulla Town, which was captured by 1600 hrs.

The Battalion continued its advance. It reached Rampur (Buniyar) by 10 November, and captured Uri by 13 November. The retreating raiders blew off all bridges enroute, but this did not hinder the Battalion's advance, as they crossed over the affected nalas/streams using makeshift arrangements of temporary bridges. Post this, defensive picquets were established at Uri to cover the road leading from Uri to Domel. Meanwhile, the regular Pakistan Army was by now fully committed to battle, and adopted an aggressive posture. A number of heavy engagement took place between the enemy and 1 SIKH. As a part of the defenses, 1 SIKH had established a small picquet anointed 'Nalwa') across the Jhelum River that was held by 20 men, and commanded by a JCO. It was this isolated post, difficult to reinforce, on which the enemy attacked repeatedly on the night of 22/23 November. One of the fiercest fighting took place at the Nalwa Picquet where about 400 enemy personnel were killed.

Skirmishes with the enemy continued on a daily basis from 10 December. The tempo of activities suddenly increased. The raiders concentrated on the high hills overlooking Uri from the South-East and posed a threat to Uri. 1 SIKH, now concentrated at Uri was tasked to dislodge the enemy from there. On 12 December, the Battalion was launched to clear Bhatgiran, which saw fierce fighting as part of a frontal daylight attack.

Jemadar Nand Singh, VC, led his platoon into a well-entrenched enemy position. After a desperate fight, his platoon was able to extricate Bravo Company, which was pinned down by the enemy with concentrated fire. Though severely wounded, he continued to lead till he was killed by a burst of LMG fire. His repeated acts of valour, leadership and devotion to duty won him a posthumous Maha Vir Chakra. Thus, Jemadar Nand Singh, a military legend, passed away as one of the highest decorated soldiers of the Commonwealth. Heavy losses were inflicted on the enemy (of whom 300 were killed and 500 wounded). The unit also suffered casualties (one officer, two JCOs and 57 Other Ranks killed and two officers, including the CO, four JCOs and 55 Other Ranks wounded).

To perpetuate the memory of this gallant warrior, the main bridge over the Uri Nala, has been named as 'Nand Singh Bridge' and the road to Salamabad as 'Nand Singh Road'. An imposing memorial has been built here overlooking the bridge. Meanwhile, as this was going on, Subedar Bishan Singh, Military Cross, of Delta Company launched a counter-attack, and in severe hand-to-hand fighting, threw the enemy back. Even as more waves of enemy troops were thrown into battle, Bishan Singh continued to cheer and encourage his men, and was killed in action. His cool courage, inspiring leadership and dogged determination against heavy odds earned him a posthumous Maha Vir Chakra. The gallant actions of the Sikh Regt in the 1947-48 Kashmir Operations were specially commended by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru: -

"The Sikh Regiment played a gallant role in the Kashmir Operations and reached Srinagar just in time to save the country from the aggressor. Valour and spirit of sacrifice displayed by the men of the Sikh Regiment were examples worthy of emulate."
