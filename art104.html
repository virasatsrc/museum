Saga of Saragarhi and Northern China

Title: Saga of Saragarhi and Northern China
Category: Saga of Saragarhi and Northern China
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
During the frontier uprisings of 1897, Sikh battalions fought at Malakand, Chakdara and Tirah. Their supreme test came at Saragarhi on 12 September. Havildar Ishar Singh, twenty Sikh soldiers and one non-combatant held the isolated signalling post between Forts Lockhart and Cavagnari against more than 20,000 Afridi and Orakzai tribesmen. Signaller Gurmukh Singh transmitted the battle by heliograph until the defenders were overrun. The post fell at 1630 hours; all defenders died, while the attackers suffered an estimated 450 casualties. Each of the twenty-one soldiers received the Indian Order of Merit posthumously, and 12 September became the Sikh Regiment's Regimental Day.

XIV Sikhs later served during the Boxer Rebellion and received "China 1900-02." XLVII Sikhs served at Tientsin and Lutai from 1904, earning praise for discipline and efficiency. By the eve of the First World War, the Sikh battalions had accumulated honours including Lucknow, Arrah, China, Ali Masjid, Afghanistan, Kandahar, Suakin, Tofrek, Manipur, Chitral, Saragarhi, Malakand, Tirah and the Punjab Frontier, establishing valour as a continuous regimental tradition.
