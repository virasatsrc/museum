19 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 19 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

19 Sikh was raised at Secunderabad on 1 October 1966 under the leadership of Lt Col Bhagat Singh. The battalion was presented its Colours by the President of India, Dr Zakir Hussain, in 1968.

During the 1971 Indo-Pak War, the unit was inducted for Operation Cactus Lily in the Ajnala Sector (International Border) on 16 October 1971 and took up defences in the Darya Mansur Enclave. The border outposts of Darya Mansur and Budhai Cheema were of paramount tactical significance.

On 6 December 1971, Pakistan's 34 Frontier Force launched two attacks on the battalion's positions, both preceded by intense artillery fire. Both attacks were successfully repulsed by the Khalsas. Hand grenades were exchanged at close quarters and war cries resounded over the battlefield. The determined leadership of the post commanders and the tenacity of the troops enabled the battalion to withstand the onslaught, forcing the enemy to retreat and leave behind its dead.

Later, Operation Vijay was launched to occupy the Pak Shahpur Enclave, and the task was completed without any loss of life. The final tally of the war was 45 Pakistani troops killed and 69 wounded, as against two own martyrs who laid down their lives in defence of the motherland. After this operation, the battalion earned the sobriquet "Vijays."

Since 1971, the unit has served in various tenures in both Jammu & Kashmir and the Northeast, undertaking counter-insurgency operations and high-altitude area tenures, and has consistently performed with distinction.

During peace tenures, the battalion has also lived up to its reputation through excellence in training, sports and administrative activities.

In 2013, the unit was awarded the GOC-in-C Southern Command Unit Appreciation.
