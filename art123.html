Modernisation and Regimental Identity

Title: Modernisation and Regimental Identity
Category: Modernisation and Regimental Identity
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
The active battalions were reorganised on the pattern of British rifle battalions to improve tactical independence, mobility and firepower. Units serving in the North-West Frontier Province retained mule transport, while those in the plains received motor transport. Signal platoons and eight Bren guns were mounted on carriers. In 1929, each battalion's Delta Company became a machine-gun company equipped with six Vickers machine guns, its strength rising from thirty-six to eighty-seven men. The battalions retained a composition of roughly two-thirds Sikhs and one-third Punjabi Muslims.

The same year, steel chakkars were authorised for wear on the turban. Each was engraved with the number of its soldier's rifle and returned when he retired. In 1923, the crest of 5/11 Sikh was redesigned after the Duke of Connaught became its Colonel-in-Chief, with the ducal coronet replacing the King's crown and the title "Duke of Connaught's Own" placed on the scroll.
