14 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 14 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

14 Sikh was raised by Lt Col H. S. Sidhu on 1 April 1989. During its first tenure at Siliguri, the battalion ensured the security of the area and played a major role in breaking the backbone of ULFA. Subsequently, during its tenure in Kashmir, the battalion made its mark by eliminating the District Commander of Hizbul Mujahideen along with three other hardcore terrorists. It also played an important role in Operation Charar-e-Sharif. In 1996, Gen Shankar Roy Chaudhary, Chief of Army Staff, presented the battalion with its Colours.

During Operation Vijay in 1999, the entire battalion, without acclimatisation, was airlifted from Delhi to the battlefield in Batalik at an altitude of approximately 18,000 feet in a record time of 24 hours. The battalion successfully captured Point 5310 and Point 5512, located about 2.5 km inside enemy territory. For its distinguished performance, the battalion proudly came to identify itself as the "Saviours of Chorbat La."

During its tenure at Ranchi, the battalion's continuous hard work and perseverance were recognised when it became the proud recipient of the "Cockerel" Banner for two years, in 2007-08 and 2009-10.

At Drass in High Altitude Area, the battalion significantly enhanced the security apparatus through the construction of field defences and vehicle tracks, while ensuring zero casualties in one of the most inhospitable terrains.

At Meerut in 2015, the battalion's hard work earned it the GOC-in-C, Western Command Unit Appreciation. During its tenure at Tamei, Manipur, while operating in a counter-insurgency environment, the battalion successfully arrested 15 hardcore terrorists.

At Jalandhar, the unit was tasked with flood relief operations in Punjab. In a vast area of approximately 90 square kilometres, beginning on 19 August 2019, the indomitable spirit and determination of the Khalsas enabled the evacuation of nearly 5,000 civilians from 21 flood-prone villages within just six days. The operation was highly appreciated by the military hierarchy as well as the civil administration.
