17 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 17 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

17 Sikh (Tigers) was raised on 1 February 1948 at Ambala Cantt by Lt Col N. J. M. Pettengell, MC. The battalion was formed from Sikh companies of 3/1, 1/8 and 4/8 Punjab Regiments, and 4/13, 5/13 and 6/13 (Royal) Frontier Force Rifles.

On 17 March 1954, while the unit was taking part in an exercise at Shivpuri, Madhya Pradesh, it was tasked by the civil administration to end the menace of a man-eating tigress. L/Nk Sucha Singh killed the tigress with his bayonet. In recognition of this brave act, the battalion was christened "Tigers."

The battalion has participated in all major operations, earning accolades for the Regiment, the Army and the Nation through its outstanding resolve and professionalism. These operations include Operation Polo (1948), during which the State of Hyderabad was integrated into India; all the Indo-Pak Wars; the Sino-India War of 1962; Operation Pawan in Sri Lanka; and the prolonged armed stand-off with China in Doklam as part of Operation Juniper, besides deployment along the Northern Borders under Operation Falcon.

The battalion has also remained at the forefront of sports and adventure activities and has produced several national and international sporting stalwarts and accomplished mountaineers. Notable sportsmen include Nb Sub Gurcharan Singh, an Olympian in boxing; Hav Gobind Singh, an Asian Games athlete; Sub Maj & Hony Capt Dayal Singh, an Asian Games athlete; Hav Gurinder Singh, an international rowing champion; and Sub Daljit Singh, an international shooting champion.

Late Col J. C. Joshi, Arjuna Awardee (Retd), was a member of the first Indian Army expedition to Mount Everest in 1965. Col D. N. Tankha, AVSM (Retd) led successful expeditions to Nun Kun Peak in the Zanskar Range in June 1971 and Mount Sickle Moon in July 1973 while serving as the battalion's Commanding Officer.

The battalion has proved its mettle time and again in various operations. Its gallant actions have been recognised through numerous awards in both the pre- and post-Independence eras.
