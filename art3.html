GURU AMAR DAS JI

Guru Amar Das Ji was born on 05 May 1479 at Basarke, near Amritsar to Tej Bhan and Mata Lachmi. He became Guru at 73 years of age and showed great devotion in taking forward the work of earlier two Gurus. He helped to transform Goindwal, which was a new settlement, into a centre of Sikh devotion. He made 'Langar' an integral institution of Sikh faith by insisting that anyone who wanted to see him had to first accept his hospitality by eating with the disciples. Even Emperor Akbar, who visited Goindwal in 1567, willingly ate in the langar. His far-reaching reforms included promotion of caste and gender equality, abolition of Sati and encouraging re-marriage of widows. Guru Amar Das took the first step towards construction of Sri Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) by choosing its site, although the temple was built several decades later. His devoted disciple Jetha was chosen as his successor and re-named as Ram Das ('Servant of the Lord'). Guru Amar Das died at Goindwal on 01 September 1574 at the age of ninety five.

'He made divine knowledge his steed and chastity his saddle. On the bar of truth He strung the arrow of God's praise. In the age of utter darkness, He rose like the Sun. He sowed the seed of truth and reaped its fruit'
Adi Granth, applauding Guru Amar Das' work
