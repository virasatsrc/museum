Lebanon and the Blue Line

Title: Lebanon and the Blue Line
Category: Lebanon and the Blue Line
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
The next major deployment came in November 2002, when 8 Sikh joined the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon. Chosen after its performance at Tiger Hill during Operation VIJAY, the battalion assumed responsibility for 380 square kilometres, including nineteen posts, forty-eight villages and fifty kilometres of the Blue Line between Israel and Hizbollah. Its soldiers monitored air violations, shelling, stone-throwing and disputes over the River Hasbani, while maintaining contact with rival forces and assisting local communities through medical services, water supply, environmental work and computer education. It also won the inter-contingent shooting competition twice and the ten-kilometre run.

A company of 4 Mechanised Infantry (1 Sikh) served in Congo in 2004. On 15 December 2005, 4 Sikh took over approximately sixty-two kilometres of the Lebanese Blue Line as INDBATT-VIII. When fighting erupted on 12 July 2006 and continued until the UN-brokered ceasefire of 14 August, its posts endured close artillery and air attacks, damaged communications and severe logistic shortages. The battalion maintained observation, evacuation and humanitarian tasks throughout. After Patrol Base Khiam was destroyed by an air strike, one officer and twelve other ranks joined the dangerous recovery operation for the missing UN observers. The battalion provided food, water, medicines, shelter and assistance in restoring essential services. Its service brought a Sena Medal (Distinguished), the UNIFIL Force Commander's Unit Citation on 24 August 2006, eighty-one Commendation Cards and a Lebanese War Medal.
