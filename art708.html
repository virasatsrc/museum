8 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 8 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

8 Sikh was initially raised on 1 May 1941 at Nowshera by Lt Col A. R. Walker. It was converted into Light Anti-Aircraft Artillery on 31 March 1942. Following the widespread expansion of the Indian Army after 1962, the battalion commenced its re-raising on 1 January 1963 at Meerut Cantt under the command of Lt Col Jaswant Singh Sandhu.

The Colours of the battalion were presented by Dr Zakir Husain, President of India, on 2 March 1968.

The battalion took part in Operation Cactus Lily in December 1971. On 6 December 1971, it captured the formidable enemy post Jhandi Mal at approximately 16,000 feet, after fierce hand-to-hand combat and successfully beating back a Pakistani counter-attack.

The unit was inducted into Operation Vijay in Drass on 14 May 1999, with barely two days of acclimatisation and minimum essential clothing. It was tasked to capture the strategic and seemingly impregnable Tiger Hill. For its success, the battalion was awarded the Battle Honour "Tiger Hill" and the Theatre Honour "Kargil." It also received the coveted COAS Unit Citation and a unique citation from the Punjab Government for its distinguished and gallant actions.

While operating in Assam from 2007 to 2010, the battalion participated in Operation Samatal-II and created history by liberating five villages from insurgency, eliminating terrorists, and effecting massive recoveries of IEDs and warlike stores. The battalion's Ghatak Platoon represented Eastern Command on three occasions in the Cambrian Patrol Army Championship at Commando School, Belgaum.

For its exceptional performance at Torna (LC-HAA), Jammu & Kashmir, the battalion was awarded the GOC-in-C Northern Command Unit Citation in February 2016.

During its tenure at Chandimandir, the "Charhdi Kala" Battalion won the coveted 9 Infantry Division Overall Banner for two consecutive years.
