THE ANGLO-SIKH WARS

In the forty years of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's rule, the British had triumphed over all the other powers - the Nawabs of Bengal and Oudh, the Rajputs, Rohillas, Jats and Gurkhas; they had also vanquished the Maratha and Mughal powers - the only independent Indian Kingdom that remained was that of the Punjab. But within a decade of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's death in 1839, the administrative set up of the Lahore Durbar was reduced to chaos. The army swelled to three times its size but was devoid of discipline or direction. The internecine fights, machinations of murder and intrigue spilled out into the streets and virtual Civil War ensued between feuding families.

The British, who had prepared a blueprint of invasion in early 1844, and by the winter of 1845, had begun to move their Infantry and Artillery along the Satluj River for military action against the Khalsa Armies.
