Reorganisation and Re-equipment

Title: Reorganisation and Re-equipment
Category: Reorganisation and Re-equipment
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
The reverses of 1962 made clear that national security required a larger, better-trained and properly equipped Army. India therefore began a major reorganisation, raising six new infantry divisions and preparing both new and existing formations for mountain warfare. In 1963, mountain divisions received modern equipment, including the 7.62 mm self-loading rifle, 81 mm mortar, .30 Browning machine gun and improved artillery. The Sikh Regiment expanded simultaneously, adding 6 Sikh, 7 Sikh, 8 Sikh and 9 Sikh.

The Sikh Regimental Centre, organised in peace to train only 800 recruits, suddenly received nearly 5,000 men. Tents, verandas and other improvised accommodation were used to meet the demand. Lieutenant Colonel Shamsher Singh, the Commandant, and his Adjutant, also named Shamsher Singh, provided the necessary leadership, while experienced soldiers such as Major Prakash Singh, Victoria Cross, organised training. The expansion had barely begun to consolidate when renewed tension with Pakistan led towards war in 1965.
