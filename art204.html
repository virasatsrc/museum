NAIK GIAN SINGH, VC

Title: Naik Gian Singh, VC
Category: Hall of Fame
Award: Victoria Cross
Era / Action: Second World War, 1945
Citation (Written):

Naik Gian Singh, VC (Second World War, 1945) On 02 March 1 945, the Japanese were holding a strong position astride the road Kamye-Myingyan. Two companies of the 15 PUNJAB REGIMENT successfully carried out a wide encircling movement and established themselves on a high ground about one and a half mile in the rear of the enemy position. As all water supply points were within the enemy position it was vital that the enemy be dislodged. Naik Gian Singh was in command of the leading section which was ordered to attack the position. The enemy was well concealed in foxholes along cactus hedges. Naik Gian Singh soon observed enemy at about 20 yards ahead. Ordering his Light Machine Gun to cover him, he alone rushed the enemy fox-holes, firing his Tommy Gun. He was met by a hail of fire and was wounded in the arm. In spite of this he continued his advance alone, hurling grenades and killing several Japanese. A troop of tanks, which moved up in support of this platoon came under fire from a concealed enemy Anti-Tank Gun. Naik Gian Singh saw the threat to the tanks and ignoring the danger to himself and despite his wounds, rushed forward, killed the crew and captured the gun single-handed. His section followed him down a lane of cactus hedges, clearing all enemy positions which were being firmly held. Some 20 enemy bodies were found in this area, the majority of which fell to Naik Gian Singh and his section. The magnificent gallantry of this Naik throughout the action and his devotion to duty and leadership could not have been surpassed.
