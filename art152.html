The Indo-Sino War, 1962

Title: The Indo-Sino War, 1962
Category: The Indo-Sino War, 1962
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
Growing tension along the Himalayan frontier led India to adopt a Forward Policy and deploy troops in NEFA. 1 Sikh arrived in May 1961 and later moved to the Tawang sector. When the Chinese attacked Dhola on 20 October 1962, Captain Mahabir Prasad, serving with the Assam Rifles, fought to the death and received the Maha Vir Chakra posthumously.

At dawn on 23 October, repeated Chinese assaults struck the isolated IB Ridge post commanded by Subedar Joginder Singh. His platoon repulsed successive waves despite mounting casualties. Wounded in the thigh, he continued directing the defence and finally led the survivors in a bayonet charge. When the position was overrun, artillery was called onto the post itself. Joginder Singh later died of his wounds in captivity and was posthumously awarded the Param Vir Chakra, the only such award in NEFA. His ashes were returned by the Chinese in May 1963.

At Tong Peng La, Delta Company under Lieutenant Hari Pal Kaushik, supported by Havildar Sarup Singh's section, defeated enveloping attacks by a much larger force before withdrawing under orders to Milaktonga and then Sela by 26 October. At Sela, Charlie Company repulsed an assault at Dzajirong La on 10 November. A reconnaissance in force on 15 November was ambushed, and during the withdrawal of 17-18 November, 1 Sikh fought while ammunition ran out. Second Lieutenant Surendra Dagur, Lance Naik Pritam Singh and Sepoy Naurang Singh distinguished themselves, while the commanding officer, Lieutenant Colonel B.N. Mehta, was killed.
