Fauj-i-Ain and European Reorganisation

Title: Fauj-i-Ain and European Reorganisation
Category: Fauj-i-Ain and European Reorganisation
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
Recognising that traditional fight-run-fight tactics alone could not defeat disciplined infantry and artillery, Ranjit Singh introduced European drill, organisation and weapons. Several trained battalions existed by 1812. Jean-François Allard and Jean-Baptiste Ventura entered his service in 1822, followed by Claude Auguste Court and Paolo Avitabile in 1827. The Fauj-i-Khas, called the "French Legion" by British intelligence, contained infantry, cavalry and artillery, while the larger Kampu-i-Mu'alla remained under Punjabi officers. The Fauj-i-Ain expanded from 4,061 men in 1811 to 70,721 in 1845, and by 1839 British intelligence estimated the Punjab Army at 150,000 men.
