Title: Defence of Chhamb (1971)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

5 SIKH under Lieutenant Colonel Prem Khanna, was deployed at Chhamb in a Brigade Group along with 5 ASSAM and 4/1 GORKHA RIFLES. 5 SIKH defences covered Point 303, Phagla and Mandiala, all located West of Munawar Tawi. At the beginning of December 1971, Pakistan Artillery and Air Force were very active in the areas of 5 SIKH and 5 ASSAM. Pakistan had employed its 23 Infantry Division with three Brigades, three Armoured Regiments, plus numerous additional units, including Mujahids, Scouts, Militia and Rangers, for operations. Their aim was to capture Chhamb by a swift armoured thrust, crossing the Munawar Tawiand capturing all territory up to Akhnoor. The Indian plan initially was also to take up an offensive in the same sector.

The Pakistan Offensive was launched at 2050 hrs on 03 December 1971. It concentrated heavy bombardment on Pir Jamal and Moel which were ahead of 5 SIKH main defences. Heavy fighting took place at these positions and troops at Moel repulsed three attacks by night, inflicting heavy casualties on the enemy. However, with very few survivors to fight on, they were finally forced back by repeated armoured assaults, supported by the infantry. This resilient action of holding the enemy at bay for more than 18 hours, put the enemy's time plan completely out of gear, and gained critical hours for improving defences in the depth areas. By mid-night, the enemy also attacked Phagla, Gurha and Mandiala, and contacted Point 303. The tanks deployed there fell back to Mandiala South and held on to this position till the evening of 06 December, against heavy odds. Phagla and Point 303 were again attacked on 04 and 05 December, but all attacks were beaten back most valiantly, with heavy casualties on the enemy. However, the posts had to finally fall back and withdraw East of Munawar Tawi.

The enemy was able to infiltrate across Munawar Tawi and some of its troops reached the bridge over the river. As this posed a threat to this vital link, it became expedient to re-capture the Chhamb Bridge. A quick reflex action by own infantry and armoured elements took the Pakistani troops by surprise, and the bridge was taken back in a swift action. A number of subsequent counter-attacks by the enemy were also foiled. On the afternoon of 05 December, the enemy made several attempts to capture Phagla and Point 303. These positions were defended by Alpha Company, whose gallant commander, Major DS Pannu, continuously beat back enemy's battalion strength attacks over two nights, till he was mortally wounded by a shell. His martyrdom inspired his men to fight tenaciously despite the lack of rest and sleep. 5 SIKH withstood the continuous enemy infantry and armour assaults and successfully beat back most of these attacks, inflicting heavy casualties on the enemy.

On 06 December, Point 303 exchanged hands within four hours. The Pakistani troops captured it, but were soon to face a quick counterattack led by Captain Kamal Bakshi. Three hours later, Point 303 was recaptured by 5 SIKH, an action in which the brave officer laid down his life. The enemy fled, leaving behind 70 dead, two Medium Machine Guns and 70 rifles, and the position was stabilised. Phag La defended locality was attacked next in overwhelming numbers by Pakistan Army - this position was under the unshakeable command of Major (later Colonel) Jaivir Singh. In intense hand-to-hand fighting, the heavy enemy force was beaten back. However, during the night of 06/07 December, 5 SIKH was pulled back to a position in depth. After Pakistan's failure to capture any territory across Munawar Tawi, it decided to consolidate defences on the Western side, before the cease-firecame into effect on 17 December 1971.

The battles fought in the open, undulating ground of Phagla Point 303 were short, but very intense. In these 72 hours, 5 SIKH valiantly defended an area, where Pakistan employed infantry and armoured forces in much superior numbers - almost two infantry brigades and two armoured regiments launched incessant attacks in overwhelming strength across the battalion's frontage, allowing no rest to its beleaguered troops. The positions occupied by 5 SIKH were continuously softened either by infantry and armour attacks, or artillery and air strikes. The Sikhs displayed their resilience to remain in a state of high morale and fight over a prolonged period. The Pakistan Army had evidently forgotten the level of motivation, capability and capacity of the Sikh troops to withstand such continuous assaults.

Lieutenant Colonel Prem Khanna, CO and Major Jaivir Singh were later awarded the Maha Vir Chakra, while Lance Naik Richhpal Singh and Major DS Pannu were awarded the Vir Chakra, the latter posthumously. 5 SIKH also received the Theatre Honour - 'Jammu and Kashmir-1971' for its distinguished performance in operations.
