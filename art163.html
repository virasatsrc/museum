Positioning for the Coming Conflict

Title: Positioning for the Coming Conflict
Category: Positioning for the Coming Conflict
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
By October 1964, 1 Sikh had returned to its former battlefield at Chamkot in the Tangdhar sector. Lieutenant Colonel R.W. Emery assumed command in July 1965, when the battalion also received 7.62 mm self-loading rifles. 2 Sikh was deployed in the newly activated "S" Sector of the Indo-Tibet border, manning picquets as high as 17,000 feet after a difficult four-day march from Shimla. In August 1965, under Lieutenant Colonel N.N. Khanna, it moved to Jammu.

After Operation VIJAY, 3 Sikh returned to Ambala and moved in August 1962 to hold the ceasefire line in the Gurez-Kanzalwan sectors. In December 1963, 4 Sikh entered Nagaland for Operation ORCHID, conducting continuous combing operations against Naga hostiles before moving to Ferozepur in July 1965. The post-Independence battalions, 16 Sikh, 17 Sikh and 18 Sikh, had missed the 1962 war. During the Rann of Kutch confrontation in April 1965, 18 Sikh mobilised at short notice, first moving to the East Pakistan border and then to northern Sikkim. By mid-1965, the Regiment's battalions were positioned across the country, ready for the conflict that was about to begin.
