Edinburgh and Later Achievements

Title: Edinburgh and Later Achievements
Category: Edinburgh and Later Achievements
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
In 1962, the Sikh Regimental Centre Brass Band and 2 Sikh Pipes and Drums, led by Drum Major Gurdial Singh, represented India at the Edinburgh Military Tattoo. They performed at Tynecastle Park on 4 August and at St Paul's Cathedral, London, on 12 September. Their rendering of "Scotland the Brave," soldierly bearing and distinctive uniforms won a standing ovation and praise from Field Marshals Sir Claude Auchinleck and Sir William Slim.

The Band visited Oman in 1971 under Subedar Kashmir Singh. Sikh pipers later participated in European remembrance ceremonies in October 2003, while regimental bands served under the UN in Lebanon and the Democratic Republic of Congo. A performance at Thimphu in 2007 marked the fifty-second birthday of Bhutan's Fourth Druk Gyalpo, Jigme Singye Wangchuck.

On Republic Day 1979, 2 Sikh Pipes and Drums performed in New Delhi and won the first Army inter-band competition, being declared best band under Army Order 83/79. Regimental bands subsequently appeared at the South Asian Federation Games in 1987, Rashtrapati Bhavan in 1989, 1998 and 2005, the Commonwealth Games opening on 4 October 2010, and the Army Day and Beating the Retreat ceremonies in January 2011.

Bandmasters such as Subedar Gulab Singh, Drum Major Gurdial Singh, Naib Subedar Darshan Singh and Havildar Major Sunder Singh shaped this tradition. Gulab Singh also composed the music for the regimental song Deh Shiva Bar Mohi Ehae, based principally on the ragas Bhairavi, Aarohi and Bilaval. The bands preserved the Regiment's martial rhythm and cultural identity.
