From King's Colours to the Republic

Title: From King's Colours to the Republic
Category: From King's Colours to the Republic
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
After Independence, the old King's Colours were laid to rest at the Indian Military Academy, Dehradun, on 23 November 1950. Major General K.S. Thimayya received them in a ceremony attended by General K.M. Cariappa, Lieutenant General Sir Archibald Nye and Defence Minister Sardar Baldev Singh.

A decisive new chapter opened at Meerut on 2 March 1968, when President Dr Zakir Hussain presented Colours to the Sikh Regimental Centre and fourteen battalions. The 1,300-strong parade was commanded by Colonel Gurbaksh Singh. The old Colours were trooped through the ranks, marched off to "Auld Lang Syne" and placed in the Quarter Guard before the new Colours were consecrated and presented. It was the first time the Regiment had received Colours from India's Head of State.

As the Regiment expanded, General V.N. Sharma, Chief of Army Staff, presented Colours to 13, 20, 21 and 22 Sikh at Ramgarh Cantonment on 3 March 1989. The parade was commanded by Brigadier Trilochan Singh under Lieutenant General P.S. Vadehra, Colonel of the Regiment. Finally, on 19 October 1996, General Sankar Roy Chowdhury presented Colours to 11 Sikh and 14 Sikh at the Maharaja Ranjit Singh Parade Ground. The ceremony also honoured fourteen war widows, including Joginder Kaur, widow of Jemadar Nand Singh, VC and MVC. Through each presentation, the Colours linked successive generations to the Regiment's record of valour, sacrifice and duty.
