1 SIKH (4 MECH INF)

Sikh Regiment / 4 Mechanised Infantry

Unit Name: 1 SIKH (4 MECH INF)
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: Sikh Regiment / 4 Mechanised Infantry

Detailed Historical Record

Raised on 30 July 1846 as the "Regiment of Ferozepore" from remnants of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's Army.

The battalion contributed with great distinction as part of the Colonial Army and was conferred with 10 Battle Honours during the Great War.

In the Second World War, the battalion saw success in Burma and helped curb Japanese aggression. It was bestowed with seven Battle Honours, one Theatre Honour and one Victoria Cross.

The unit was the first to be airlifted to quell Kurdish tribesmen on 21 February 1923. It also had the rare distinction of being the only unit to give the first salute to the National Flag at both the Red Fort and India Gate on 15 August 1947.

It was the first unit to be flown to Srinagar on 27 October 1947, earning the sobriquet "Saviours of Kashmir". This day is commemorated as Infantry Day. Lt Col D R Rai, CO, was awarded Independent India's first Maha Vir Chakra.

In 1948, the battalion earned the Battle Honour "Tithwal". During the battle, L/Nk (later Sub & Hony Capt) Karam Singh, Military Medal recipient, was awarded the Param Vir Chakra, while Jem Nand Singh, VC, MVC, one of the most decorated soldiers of the Commonwealth, laid down his life.

During the Indo-Sino War of 1962, the battalion once again proved its mettle by holding its positions initially at Bum La and later at Sela. Sub Joginder Singh was awarded the Param Vir Chakra posthumously.

On 2 April 1981, the battalion converted to the Mechanised Infantry Regiment and received the Mechanised Infantry Colours from the President on 24 February 1988.

It became the first unit to be bestowed with the honour and title "Bravest of the Brave" by the Chief of Army Staff on 15 January 1997.

The unit has the distinction of being the most decorated unit of the Indian Army.
