Anglo-Sikh Wars

Title: Anglo-Sikh Wars
Category: Anglo-Sikh Wars
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
The First Anglo-Sikh War began in 1845. At Mudki on 18 December, Sikh resistance surprised the British, but treachery among senior leaders prevented success. At Ferozeshah on 21-22 December, the British came close to annihilation during the "night of terror." The Sikhs offered limited resistance at Aliwal on 29 January 1846 before the decisive Battle of Sabraon on 10 February. Sikh soldiers stood under intense artillery fire and fought without seeking quarter, but Tej Singh abandoned the field and disrupted the bridge over the Sutlej. Sham Singh Attariwala, dressed in white and resolved to die, repeatedly rallied his men and fell among them.

British control of Lahore and the disbandment of the Khalsa Army created widespread anger. In the Second Anglo-Sikh War, Sher Singh Attariwala selected a strong position at Chillianwala on 13 January 1849, while Jawahir Singh Nalwa led a cavalry charge that drove British dragoons from the field. The British suffered 2,357 casualties and lost three standards, but the Sikhs failed to exploit their advantage. Reinforced British forces won the Battle of Gujarat in February, and the war ended on 14 March 1849 with the annexation of Punjab.
