The Sikh Regiment's insignia preserves this inheritance. Its centrepiece is the chakkar associated with Guru Gobind Singh and the Khalsa, enclosing a lion representing "Singh," while the State Emblem above symbolises power, courage, pride and confidence. With eighteen regular infantry battalions and three battalions each of the Rashtriya Rifles and Territorial Army affiliated to it, the Regiment became the largest in its 165-year history.

Its 2,749 gallantry awards include two Param Vir Chakras, three Ashoka Chakras, fourteen Maha Vir Chakras and sixty-eight Vir Chakras. It also holds more than one hundred Battle and Theatre Honours. Eleven battalions have received COAS Unit Citations and ten units GOC-in-C Unit Appreciations. The senior battalions, 4 Mechanised Infantry (1 Sikh) and 2 Sikh, bear the distinction "Bravest of the Brave." Sustained by discipline, faith and Charhdi Kala, the Sikh soldier continues the Warrior-Saint tradition and remains prepared to sacrifice his life for the nation.
