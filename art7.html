GURU HAR RAI JI

Guru Har Rai was born at Kiratpur on 16 January 1630 to Baba Gurditta and Mata Nihal Kaur. He is notable for maintaining the large Sikh Army that his predecessor had created, yet avoiding military conflict. Guru Har Rai was soft-spoken and religious minded - he believed in truth and non-violence. He strictly adhered to the routine of a life of prayer as exhorted by Guru Nanak. He lived for almost 14 years in Sirmoor State and undertook several tours to major Sikh centres in Malwa and Doaba regions; alongside, his emissaries travelled across Eastern India, Rajasthan, Kashmir and Kabul to spread Guru Nanak's teachings - many families were brought into the Sikh fold through these efforts. During the Mughal war of succession, Guru Har Rai sided with the moderate Dara Shikoh which earned him Aurangzeb's ire later. One of his son's, Ram Rai built a small community centre which has transformed into the present-day Dehradun. Before dying at Kiratpur on 06 October 1661, Guru Har Rai anointed his youngest son, Har Krishan as the next Sikh Guru.

'...man breaks flowers with one hand and offers them with the other, but the flowers perfume both hands alike. The axe cuts the sandal tree, yet the sandal perfumes the axe'
Guru Har Rai's response to his followers who questioned why he was helping Dara Shikoh, whose father Shah Jahan had been hostile to Sikh Gurus
