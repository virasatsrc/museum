3 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 3 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

3 Sikh was raised at Lahore in January 1856 as the Bengal Military Police Battalion by Captain Thomas Rattray.

At the time of its raising, the battalion had a mixed composition comprising ten infantry companies of 100 men each, along with a troop of 200 cavalrymen.

In 1864, the unit was rechristened and designated as 45th Rattray's Sikh and became a full-fledged infantry battalion.

In 1885, it was granted pure Sikh status. Its present designation dates back to 1947.

The battalion participated in both World Wars under the Colonial Army and was awarded 10 Battle Honours and five Theatre Honours.

3 Sikh is one of the oldest infantry battalions of the Sikh Regiment, with a glorious history of valour, distinguished performance in operations including counter-insurgency operations, and excellence in sports and professional competitions.

The unit also has a unique tradition of salutation: "Wahe Guru Ji Ki Fateh."

Post-Independence, in recognition of its bravery and excellent operational performance, the battalion has been awarded two COAS Unit Citations for its consecutive field tenures in Rajouri Sector in 2003 and Assam in 2011.
