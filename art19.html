SHAM SINGH ATTARIWALA

Sardar Sham Singh Attariwala (1790-1846) was another key General of the Khalsa Army. He joined the Sikh military in 1817, and Maharaja Ranjit Singh, knowing his qualities and fighting abilities, made him a Jathedar with 5000 horsemen. He participated in the Battles of Attock, Multan, Peshawar and the Kashmir Expedition of 1819. After Maharaja Ranjit Singh's death, he became a Division Commander with 4000 Cavalry and two Infantry Brigades.

Sham Singh Attariwala took his last stand at the Battle of Sobraon (10 February 1846), which was fought during the First Anglo-Sikh War (1845-46). During the battle, he was in command of the left flank of the Khalsa Army and had taken a vow to either return victorious from Sobraon or to die in battle. As the Sikh defences were being overrun by a numerically superior British force, Sham Singh Attariwala came forward with a white robe (the colour of sacrifice) over his clothes, calling upon his troops to rally around him. He was mortally wounded by a bayonet when the hand-to-hand fighting penetrated deeper into Sikh defences. Recording his feelings about the extreme gallantry of Sham Singh Attariwala and his men, General Sir Hugh Gough, the British Commander-in-Chief at Sobraon, wrote, "I declare, were it not from a deep conviction that my country's good required the sacrifice, I would have wept to have witnessed the fearful slaughter of so devoted a body of men."

Every year at his tomb in Attari, an 'Akhand Path' is conducted, and 'Kirtan' is recited in his sacred memory - his valiant deeds are recalled, lectures on his life are delivered and 'Guru Ka Langar' is served.
