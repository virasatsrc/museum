Title: Battle of Keren (1941)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

Forming part of the 7th Indian Infantry Brigade, under the 4th Indian Division, 4/11 SIKH arrived in Cairo on 11 October 1941. On 11 January 1941, 4/11 SIKH moved for operations, to attack Kassala, 250 miles East of Khartoum, but the Italians withdrew. On 21 January, they had their first brush with the Italians at Keru Gorge, and again on 01 February, near Agordat. The first contact made by 4/11 SIKH against the enemy was in the Keren Hills, where Crack Alpine Troops had taken up defenses. Italian defenses were located on dominating features. Surrounding them and below the crest, were wide concrete trenches, with bastions for Machine Gun posts. On the reverse slope, they had placed guns and mortar emplacements, in which were strongly built dugouts for higher commanders. Further down the slopes and dug in under the huge rocks were positions for snipers and Light Machine Guns.

On 06 February 1941, 4/11SIKH moved forward 45 miles from Agordat, to a point near the Keren Hills. Here, it was learnt that the pass leading to Keren was blown up by the Italians and thus, Keren had to be approached via an alternate, narrow route, through a place called Aqua Col, which was heavily defended. On 12 February, the attack on Acqua Col was launched, with Charlie Company, commanded by Captain Hartley, and Alpha Company under Subedar Ujagar Singh. When the artillery fire lifted, the troops came under point blank fire by the enemy, and suffered heavily. Two sections managed to reach the top, bayoneting their way through. One Section Commander was killed. while the other, Naik Ujagar Singh, attacked the Machine Gun position single handedly. Though wounded, he held the position till ordered to withdraw. For this display of gallantry and leadership, Naik Ujagar Singh was awarded an Indian Order of Merit (IOM). Of the 87 men of Charlie Company that went into attack, 70 were either killed or wounded.

On 12 March, orders for another attack were received, and a fresh draft of 272 men reached the Battalion. Thus strengthened, the Battalion moved off as part of 5th Brigade to its Assembly Area on Hill 1702, its objective being Mount Samana, held by Crack Italian Alpine troops. Alpha Company, under Captain Mohammad Siddiq, already twice wounded, stromed the objectives as soon as the artillery concentration were lifted, much to the surprise of the enemy. The Military Cross for exemplary leadership in this action was best owed on Captain Siddiq, and IDSMs were awarded to Naik Daulat Singh and Lance Naik Nasib Singh. Attacks and counter attacks continued all day long. This cost the Battalion one VCO and 15 Other Ranks, plus 188 wounded, including four officers and an equal number of VCOs. But they bravely held on to the captured position.
