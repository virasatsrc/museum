GURU HAR KRISHAN JI

Guru Har Krishan Ji was born at Kiratpur on 07 July 1656 to Guru Har Rai and Mata Sulakhani. He was only five years old when his father died; he himself died of smallpox three years later on 30 March 1664, thus having the shortest reign as Guru. Due to his young age, he is also known as Bal Guru ('Child Guru'). The investiture of Guru Har Krishan did not suit Aurangzeb who wanted to play a more decisive role in Sikh affairs. He summoned the infant Guru to Delhi with the intention to arbitrate between him and his elder brother, Ram Rai. While at Delhi, Guru Har Krishan stayed at the house of Raja Jai Singh in the Raisina suburb. The temple which stands on the site is named Bangla Sahib ('the place of residence'). Many accounts of his short life highlight the seventh Guru's simplicity and humility, which endeared him to his followers. His dying words, 'Baba Bakale', indicated that the next Guru would be an older man from the village of Bakala, which happened to be his grand uncle, Tegh Bahadur, who was living in this village ever since the death of his father, Guru Hargobind in 1644.

'He who has faith gains all and he who is without faith acquires but little'
Guru Har Krishan
