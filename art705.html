5 SIKH

The Sikh Regiment

Unit Name: 5 SIKH
Category: Unit History
Affiliation: The Sikh Regiment

Detailed Historical Record

5 Sikh was raised at Sialkot on 21 January 1901 as "XLVII Sikhs." Soon after raising, the unit was employed in China to curb the Boxer Rebellion.

During the First World War, the battalion served in France, Mesopotamia and Palestine, earning 16 Battle Honours and the coveted Royal title of "Duke of Connaught's Own."

During the Second World War, the unit fought gallantly against the Japanese in Malaya, earning the Battle Honours of Kuantan and Niyor. Owing to heavy casualties suffered during the war, the unit remained in suspended animation until 7 Sikh was re-designated as 5 Sikh on 22 March 1960.

Post-Independence, the unit saw action during Operation Cactus Lily in the Chhamb Sector, where it was awarded two Maha Vir Chakras, two Vir Chakras, three Mentioned-in-Despatches, and the Theatre Honour "Chhamb (J&K)".

To counter the growing threat of Naga insurgency, 5 Sikh was deployed in a counter-insurgency role in Nagaland and was awarded one Ashoka Chakra (Class III). During its Dhana tenure from 2009 to 2011, the unit was awarded the GOC-in-C, Southern Command Unit Appreciation.

In 2015, during the natural disaster that devastated Kedarnath, the battalion carried out extensive rescue and relief operations. Its efforts saved thousands of lives, for which the unit was awarded the COAS Unit Citation and the Chief Minister, Uttarakhand Appreciation.

During its Pathankot tenure from 2015 to 2017, the unit acted as the "First Respondent" to terrorist attacks on Dinanagar Police Station and Air Force Station, Pathankot. During this period, the Commanding Officer briefed the Prime Minister and Defence Minister on the prevailing security situation.
