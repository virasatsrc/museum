Organisation and Character of the Khalsa

Title: Organisation and Character of the Khalsa
Category: Organisation and Character of the Khalsa
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
The Khalsa was organised on democratic principles. The individual Sikh formed the primary unit and, when qualified, could lead an army or a congregation. Five initiated Sikhs constituted a Panchayat in social affairs and a Fauj in military matters. Above them stood the Sarbat Khalsa, the General Assembly and supreme authority, where baptised Sikhs could speak freely. Its decisions, generally reached by consensus, became a Gurmatta and were accepted as the counsel of the Guru.

The Khalsa Army was a volunteer people's force drawn from all sections of society. Caste and former status lost importance after enrolment; leadership depended upon courage, merit, organising ability and service. Discipline arose from inner conviction, dedication to Dharma and belief in a collective destiny. Charhdi Kala, the buoyant confidence in ultimate victory, sustained morale. The Sikh soldier also followed a strict moral code, avoiding cruelty, protecting women and refusing to kill helpless enemies or fugitives.
