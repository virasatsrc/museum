Title: Battle of Hai (1917)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

The Battle of Hai River became XLV SIKHS' (RATTRAY'S SIKHS) most significant action during the First World War. On 01 February 1917, 37th Infantry Brigade, with XXXVI SIKHS on the left, and XLV SIKHS on the right, were ordered to attack the strongly held Turkish position on the banks of the Hai River. The brigade plan of attack involved two battalions moving up for assault, with the other two battalions in reserve. The first two sets of trenches were dispersed over a depth of 500 yards, with a frontage of 1000 yards. Both the Battalions arranged to attack in four waves, each with four platoons.

Fighting on the left flank, XXXVI SIKHS immediately ran into enfilade Machine Gun fire from the Turks. Within minutes of the battle having started, most of the officers and men involved in the first two waves were either killed or wounded. Turkish Machine Guns continued to play havoc with the battalions as the artillery was unable to neutralize them. Survivors of XXXVI SIKHS began to merge with XLV SIKHS on the right. In over 90 minutes of fighting, from the original 12 officers, 14 VCOs and 618 men, XXXVI SIKHS had been reduced to two officers, one VCO and 29 men. The CO, Colonel Gunning, was also wounded twice, but refused to move back.

Operating under the cover of a massive artillery bombardment, XLV SIKHS, led by Lieutenant Colonel HB Rattray, DSO, advanced to attack.The formidable phalanx of Khalsas surged forward steadily, in eight lines of double platoons, each 50 yards apart. The first two rows of Turkish defences were found to be lightly held, and were overrun with ease. Charlie and Delta Companies of XLV SIKHS then pressed on towards a prominent mud bank, which was the final objective. This proved to be a deep gorge, teeming with hundreds of Turks. The bloody skirmish that followed was at very close quarters, man to man, each trench being overwhelmed with cold steel and raw courage. Hand grenades and bayonets were used in the attack, and in the fighting that ensued for three hours, the Sikhs were reduced to a bare 15 per cent of the assaulting strength. All efforts were made by the RATTRAY'S SIKHS to ward off the attack on the left flank.

The enemy's superiority in numbers, and heavy casualties to both the battalions seemed to overwhelm them, but only temporarily. In spite of these odds, the RATTRAY'S SIKHS reached their objective. Meanwhile, the advance of XXXVI SIKHS, already held up due to heavy casualties on the left, was again subjected to close and deadly Machine Gun fire, and was forced to break the line. This dangerously exposed the left flank of XLV SIKHS, and enabled the Turks to press home a counter-attack from this direction. Hemmed in on the right flank by the Hai River, and from all other directions by thousands of Turks, the officers and men of RATTRAY'S SIKHS fought with the ferocity of trapped tigers. The casualties were indeed heavy, but every Khalsa who fell, accounted for many more Turks. The CO, Colonel Rattray, fighting shoulder-to-shoulder with his men, also made the supreme sacrifice. Besides him, four officers, seven VCOs and 280 men were wounded.

RATTRAY'S SIKHS received many awards, including the Battle Honour of 'Hai' and Theatre Honours of 'Tigris 1916' and 'Mesopotamia 1916'. Despite their heavy casualties, both the XXXVI and XLV SIKHS had inflicted equally heavy losses on the enemy, and had shaken their morale. The gallantry and stubborn qualities of the Sikhs in combat received the appreciation of the Army Commander, who personally visited both the units, to express his admiration for their magnificent fighting prowess. He also distributed immediate awards for gallantry to both the Units. Although the awards of Indian Distinguished Service Medals (IDSM) to Lance Naik Kartar Singh and Sepoy Narain Singh were well received, they did not match the gallantry that was shown. Both the Commanding Officers had fallen in the battle, and it was left to the VCOs to command the units.
