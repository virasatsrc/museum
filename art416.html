Title: Battle of Siramani (1971)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

In June 1971,4 SIKH under Lieutenant Colonel DS Sidhu was deployed in Internal Security duties at Duttapulia and Boyra, in Bengal, close to the International Border with erstwhile East Pakistan. It was part of 350 Infantry Brigade, the other units of this brigade being the 1 JAK RIFLES and 26 MADRAS. Alpha Squadron of 63 CAVALRY (T-55) and few PT-76 tanks of 45 CAVALRY were in support. On 22 November, Pakistan Air Force (PAF) Sabres strafed the Battalion's positions - the damage caused was minimal but Sepoy Nirmal Singh of Delta Company shot down a F-86 Sabre Jet with his long LMG. Flight Lieutenant (later Air Marshal and the 8th Chief of Pakistan Air Force) Parvaiz Mehdi Qureshi, ejected from the Sabre and was captured by the Battalion.

At the outbreak of full-scale war, Alpha Company, 4 SIKH under Major BS Chahal initially secured Makapur village, many kilometres inside hostile territory. The area further upto Hijuli was then dominated by aggressive patrolling, where a number of clashes took place with the enemy. The next enemy stronghold was Chaugacha, across the Kabadak River, from where the enemy constantly shelled Indian villages. 4 SIKH was ordered to advance towards Chaugacha with a squadron of 63 CAVALRY to establish contact with the enemy. The advance elements of Charlie and Delta Companies, under Major SS Duggal and Major Kanwaljit Singh, came under very effective and heavy Machine Gun, mortar and artillery fire, from enemy defences on the Kabadak River at Chaugacha. It was then decided to out flank the enemy. Alpha and Bravo Companies, under Major BS Chahal and Major RP Awasti, crossed the river a little upstream, in section strength (10 men at a time), in a leaky country boat, which was an unprecedented manoeuvre. The enemy, possibly realizing that they were being encircled, abandoned their positions and Chaugacha was secured on 23 November.

The Battalion was further ordered to capture Burinda, a formidable enemy locality and key to the defence of Jessore. On night 03/04 December, Alpha Company attacked and overran the Northern end of Burinda. Bravo Company then attacked through Alpha Company with a troop of T-55 Tanks, while Charlie Company was to carry out flanking move from the left, to cut off the enemy's route of withdrawal. The Battle for Burinda started in the early afternoon of 05 December, and raged fiercely till the following morning. 4 SIKH captured the positions together, with a large quantity of arms and ammunition. In the operation, the unit suffered numerous casualties - the three officers in the lead were wounded, but refused to be evacuated. The opposing Pakistani unit, 12 PUNJAB, doggedly defended Burinda, and suffered heavy causalities. This loss broke the back bone of Pakistan defences around Jessore; on 07 December, Pakistan's 107 Brigade began to withdraw Southwards from Jessore towards Khulna.

4 SIKH was quickly on the enemy's heels, along the axis towards Durgapur-Malanchi. Despite the heavily mined route, the infantrymen now mounted on T-55 Tanks, were racing towards Khulna, which was the most suitable defensive position in the area. To the East flowed the Bhairab River, to the West lay marshy land and to the South was the sea. The enemy's only worry was to the North where Pakistan's main defences were along the villages of Siramani, Shyamgunj and the rail-road axis cum crossing. 4 SIKH was given the task of fighting through and establish a road block near the railway line on the Jessore-Khulna Road. The aim was to threaten the rear and trap the enemy before delivering a final blow. 4 SIKH attacked at 1615 hrs on 15 December, from the ferry site, in four phases, each company capturing about 500 yards, and then firming in. They were supported by the entire Divisional Artillery for the task. Bravo Company. commanded by Major RP Awasti, was launched in Phase 1 and came under intense automatic fire from both flanks, suffering numerous casualties within the first 200 yards itself. But this did not deter the men who pressed on the attack till its objective was achieved.

In Phase 2, Charlie Company, under Major SS Duggal was launched at 1 800 hrs, to secure the area further and provide a firm base for Phase 3. The enemy defences strongly held by 6 PUNJAB and the 15 FRONTIER FORCE, were over run. The enemy attempted to evict the company, even counter-attacking with M-24 Chaffee Tanks, which were repulsed by the gallant Sikhs. Havildar Shamsher Singh, in utter disregard to his personal safety, crawled forward and knocked down a tank with a 3.5 inch Rocket Launcher at close range. After bitter fighting. Charlie Company was in full control by 0100 hrs in the morning of 16 December 1971, capturing large quantities of arms and ammunition.

In Phase 3, Delta Company under Major Kanwaljit Singh, was committed at 0200 hrs on 16 December, with the objective of establishing a road block astride the Jessore-Khulna Road. Enemy defences here were sited in concrete buildings with good fields of fire. The Sikhs pounced on the enemy in a lightning strike, and overran the defences, flushing out all remaining resistance. By 0500 hrs, Delta Company was firmly established astride the road. The mopping up of the remaining enemy went on till 0700 hrs. The last to be launched was Alpha Company, under Captain SB Gawaria, with two troops of tanks in support. Its aim was to establish a block astride the railway line by 0930 hrs on 16 December. They came under heavy enemy fire and suffered many casualties in the first few minutes. Undeterred, the company pressed forward and reached the railway line.

Thus, after 17 hours of continuous fighting, 4 SIKH had achieved its objectives, forcing a wedge through Pakistan's 107 Infantry Brigade and inflicting heavy casualties. This ultimately led to Pakistan's surrender in Khulna within 24 hours. For its gallant actions, the Battalion was awarded Battle Honour - 'Siramani' along with three Vir Chakras, two Sena Medals and a Vishist Seva Medal.
