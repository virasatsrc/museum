History of Sikh Regimental Band

Title: History of Sikh Regimental Band
Category: Beyond Battle Fd
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical

The history of a band in the Sikh Army dates back to the reign of Maharaja Ranjit Singh who trained drummers and buglers to set the pace of troops on the parade ground as well as during marches. The first bands of the SIKH Regiment were constituted alongside its raising in 1846, when XIV FEROZEPORE SIKHS at Ferozepore and XV LUDHIANA SIKHS at Ludhiana raised their bands, each comprising of 12 members who played traditional instruments like Ganar Pipes, Big Drums, Taras, Dhols and Bugles.

In 1862, Colonel CCG Ross, CB, the fifth Commanding Officer of 1 SIKH, dispatched a group of 21 Sikh Soldiers to the Bengal Native Army Centre at Calcutta (now Kolkata), to obtain training in Brass Band instruments. By 1888, the Battalion had its own Brass Band comprising of 34 members with Havildar Daler Singh as the first Band Major.

Colonel GN Channer, VC, the seventh Commanding Officer of 1 SIKH, a known musician, undertook the task of raising a Pipes and Drums Band. Under his able guidance and assistance from the Northamptonshire Pipers, the Battalion raised an 18-member Pipes and Drums Band. The next band to be raised was that of XLV RATTRAY'S in 1877 with a monthly maintenance allowance of Rupees One Hundred.

Both bands of the SIKH Regimental Centre (SRC) were raised as part of XXXVI SIKH, now 4 SIKH, on 12 April 1887, which later on merged with XXXV SIKH (the present SRC) at Nowshera, now in Pakistan. The Band continued their journey with the SRC, till it finally settled down at Ramgarh in 1976. In 1905, 3 SIKH Band participated in a tattoo event in Edinburgh Castle. The band was adjudged the Best Band of the Indian Army in a competition held at New Delhi in 1910. On 21 November 1905, 4 SIKH and its Band took part in a review held by HRH The Prince of Wales, and in November, took part in the Coronation Ceremonies of their Imperial Majesties at Delhi.

Prior to the First World War, it was a custom to have German Bandmasters. This ended with the outbreak of war, and VCOs stepped in, demonstrating great proficiency. Subedar Gulab Singh, Bandmaster of 1 SIKH, was called to Kenya to train the Kenyan Police Band and led the Indian Band Contingent during the Coronation Ceremony of 1911 in London. In 1946, the Military Band was selected for a tour of Burma and Malaya.

The Pipes and Drums Band of 2 SIKH was part of the United Nations Force at Gaza in 1962, where it participated in the Edinburgh Military Tattoo. In 1954, the Pipers and Drummers played at the Coronation of the King of Bhutan, and again in 2007 for the birthday of the Fourth Druk Gyalpo. In 1971, the Band was sent to Oman on special request of the King. In 2003, two pipers of 2 SIKH participated in the Remembrance Ceremony in Europe. The Pipes and Drums Band of the SIKH Regiment was adjudged the Best Band during the Army Day Parade on 15 January 2022. Recently, units have also raised Jazz Bands, with the 20 SIKH Jazz Band performing for the Service Chiefs in Delhi.
