Bonding at Sea and Air

Title: Bonding at Sea and Air
Category: Beyond Battle Fd
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical

The SIKH Regiment's historical link with the Navy dates back to 1888, when a torpedo boat built at Cowes for the Royal Indian Marines was christened 'The Sikh'. Another ship named 'The Sikh' was being built in 1914 which could not be completed due to the outbreak of the First World War. A third Royal Navy ship named 'HMS Sikh' was built in 1939, which actively participated in the Second World War and was sunk off Tobruk in the Mediterranean Sea on 14 September 1942. A wooden model of the ship is displayed in the gallery. During the 10th Post War Reunion in March 2000, 'INS Kirpan', a Khukri-Class Missile Corvette commissioned into the Indian Navy in 1991, was formally affiliated to the SIKH Regiment. The ship was decommissioned on 22 July 2023 and gifted to the Vietnam Navy, becoming the first active service warship India has gifted to another country.

The Regiment's affiliation with the Air Force goes back to 1922, when 1 SIKH was airlifted for operations against Kurdish Tribes in Iraq. This was the first occasion when Indian troops were airlifted into battle. In 1936, a company of 3 SIKH located at Rawalpindi was airlifted to Chitral for operations along the Frontier. In independent India, 1 SIKH was airlifted from Safdarjung to Srinagar in C-47 Dakotas of 12 Squadron, RIAF, on 27 October 1947. Infantry Day is celebrated each year to commemorate this historic feat which saved Kashmir from the invading tribal militia of Pakistan. The Squadron was formally affiliated with the SIKH Regiment on 31 October 2009 at the Sikh Regimental Centre, Ramgarh. Other operational airlifts include 4 SIKH's airlift to Walong in 1962 and 16 SIKH flown to NEFA during the Indo-China War. During the Kargil War in 1999, 14 SIKH was inducted by the IAF from Chandigarh to the Leh Sector.
