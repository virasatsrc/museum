Organisation and Prowess of the Khalsa Army

Title: Organisation and Prowess of the Khalsa Army
Category: Organisation and Prowess of the Khalsa Army
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
During the early eighteenth century, the Sikhs fought the Mughals and Afghans. Under Banda Bahadur they developed guerrilla warfare. After his death in 1716, they organised into the Budda Dal and Taruna Dal, collectively called the Dal Khalsa. In 1738, under Nawab Kapur Singh, they harassed Nadir Shah's retreating army by striking supplies and communications. Ahmad Shah Abdali's destruction of the Harmandar Sahib in 1762 intensified Sikh resistance. In 1765, under Jassa Singh Ahluwalia, they fought the Afghans near the Sutlej in their first major positional battle, and Abdali's seventh invasion failed.

The early Khalsa Army relied on mobility, endurance and flexible tactics. Sikh horsemen advanced within matchlock range, fired, withdrew to reload and returned before the enemy recovered. Their skill with the matchlock became proverbial, while swords, spears, battle-axes and the Akalis' chakkars added to their effectiveness. Through battlefield experience, the Khalsa evolved from scattered mounted bands into a confident military nation, prepared for the rise of an independent Sikh kingdom.
