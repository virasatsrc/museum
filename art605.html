Title: Re-organisation of the Indian Army (1922)
Category: Regimental History
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: 1922
In 1922, the Indian Army implemented a major Re-organisation Plan based on lessons from the First World War. This reform separated training depots from active units, grouping infantry battalions into permanent multi-battalion regiments. Each regiment comprised active service battalions and a permanent Training Battalion located at a central depot, relieving active units of recruitment and administrative duties. Accordingly, the XXXV Sikhs became the 10th Battalion, 11th Sikh Regiment, serving as the training center at Jhelum, while the other five historic battalions were re-designated as the 1st through 5th Battalions of the 11th Sikh Regiment. Tactical restructuring aligned Indian units with British rifle battalions, enhancing firepower with Vickers machine guns, Bren guns, and motor transport. Key landmarks included the introduction of pagri quoits, the redesign of the 5/11 Sikh crest, and the title of 'Royal' conferred on 2/11 Sikh for exceptional service during the 1935 Quetta earthquake, establishing a legendary identity.
