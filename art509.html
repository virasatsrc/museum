General RA Savory, KCIE, CB, DSO, MC

Title: General RA Savory, KCIE, CB, DSO, MC
Category: Regimental Icons
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical

1. Lieutenant General Sir Reginald Arthur Savory (1894–1980) is a celebrated British Indian Army officer with a lifelong, deep connection to the Sikh Regiment. Commissioned into the Indian Army in 1914, he was posted to the 14th Ferozepore Sikhs. He served with the regiment in the Middle East and during the Gallipoli Campaign of the First World War. During the Gallipoli campaign, he was severely wounded, and his life was saved by a brave Sikh sepoy named Udai Singh, who carried him to safety under intense fire. This dramatic incident forged a lifelong bond of mutual respect and affection between Savory and the Sikh soldiers he commanded.

2. He later commanded the 1st Battalion, 11th Sikh Regiment from 1937 to 1939 and rose to the rank of Lieutenant-General, commanding the 23rd Indian Division during the Second World War. In addition to his tactical leadership, Savory was a vocal and staunch advocate for Sikh customs and religious practices. He famously defended the right of Sikh soldiers to wear turbans (pagri) instead of steel helmets in combat, asserting that the turban provided ample protection and was central to the identity of a Sikh soldier. Because of his outspoken advocacy during and after his military career, he became affectionately known as "Savory of the Sikhs".

3. Following his retirement, General Savory continued to nurture his regimental ties. In March 1968, he travelled from Britain to India to attend the historic Colour Presentation Parade at Meerut, presided over by Lieutenant General Harbakhsh Singh. During this visit, he also made a personal journey to visit the family of Udai Singh, the soldier who had saved his life decades earlier on the battlefields of Gallipoli, reinforcing a bond that transcended time and geography.
