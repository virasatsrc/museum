Malaya and Burma

Title: Malaya and Burma
Category: Malaya and Burma
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical
5/11 Sikh reached Malaya in April 1941 under Lieutenant Colonel John Parkin. At Niyor on 25 January 1942, Alpha Company charged Japanese positions, inflicted heavy casualties and captured motorcycles, bicycles, a gun and mortars, disproving the myth of Japanese invincibility. The battalion received the Battle Honour "Niyor," but after the retreat to Singapore it was reduced to little more than 200 men and entered captivity following the surrender on 14 February.

1/11 Sikh arrived in Burma in March 1942. After Lieutenant Colonel McLaren was killed, the battalion cleared the Taukkyan-Pegu roadblock on 8 March with a mass bayonet charge, enabling the Allied withdrawal. Reduced from about 900 to 215 all ranks, it reached Imphal on 22 May and Kohima on 6 June. Returning to Burma in October 1943, it fought at the Admin Box and captured Buthidaung. On India Hill on 12 March 1944, Naik Nand Singh, though repeatedly wounded, captured three Japanese trenches single-handedly and received the Victoria Cross. Major John Brough received the DSO, with further IOMs, IDSMs and a Military Medal awarded for the battle.

Airlifted to Imphal on 26 April, the battalion fought at Kanglatongbi and helped break the Japanese offensive. General Bill Slim regarded 1/11 Sikh and 1/4 Gurkha as the two finest battalions of the Fourteenth Army. In 1945, 1/11 Sikh captured Sinthe and Myitche, crossed the Irrawaddy, occupied Pagan and supported the advance to Rangoon, where it joined the Victory Parade on 15 June.
