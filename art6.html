GURU HARGOBIND SAHIB JI

Guru Hargobind Sahib was born on 19 June 1595 at Amritsar to Guru Arjan and Mata Ganga. Owing to his father's tragic death, he introduced the process of militarisation to Sikhism and permitted Sikhs to accept offerings of arms and horses instead of money. Training a body of soldiers, he built a small fortress, Lohgarh ('castle of steel') in Amritsar. Across the Harmandir Sahib, he built the Akal Takht ('Throne of the Timeless God') where the congregation heard ballads extolling feats of heroism and discussed plans for military conquest. During several military encounters, the Guru's force inflicted severe damages to Mughal troops. Due to the hostile environment, Guru Hargobind was also forced to live a nomadic life away from Amritsar, during which he undertook extensive tours to communicate Sikh beliefs. Towards the end of his life, he lived in the Himalayan foothills of Eastern Punjab where he built the settlement of Kiratpur. He died peacefully on 03 March 1644 at Kiratpur after nominating his grandson, Har Rai to succeed him as the Seventh Guru.

'My rosary shall be the sword-belt and on my turban I shall wear the emblem of royalty'
Guru Hargobind Sahib
