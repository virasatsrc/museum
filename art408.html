Title: Defence of Tawang (1962)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

The Defence of Tawang was under Commander 4 Artillery Brigade, with 1 SIKH and 4 GARHWAL RIFLES, plus one Field Artillery Regiment, a Mountain Battery and a Mortar Battery in support. The Garhwalis were deployed at Tawang, to its East and the South. 1 SIKH was deployed ahead of Tawang and had the advantage of having spent some days in the construction of defences. Delta Company, under Lieutenant Hari Pal Kaushik, was occupying one of the front-line positions at Tongpen La, with a platoon, commanded by Subedar Joginder Singh, further deployed at IB Ridge. The IB Ridge post was naturally strong but being isolated, was vulnerable to multi-directional attacks. Bravo Company was on the Pamdir Ridge, leading to the Taktsang Gompa. The rest of 1 SIKH was at Milaktong La and Pengteng Tso. The rifle companies were deficient of warm clothing and had no heating arrangements in the prevailing sub-zero temperatures.

On 23 October 1962, at 0600 hrs, a wave of Chinese troops assaulted IB Ridge. To ensure that maximum casualties are inflicted on the enemy, Subedar Joginder Singh ordered his men to hold fire until they could see the enemy clearly. Indian Mountain Guns and Mortars sniffed their fire on to the approaching enemy, and there was some pause in the enemy's movement up the ridge. It was only then that Subedar Joginder Singh ordered his troops to open fire. The enemy line shimmered up the slope, only to be struck by sustained fire from the platoon, and fell apart, as they took shelter behind boulders and retraced their route.

Two more waves soon appeared from flanks of the ridge. The Chinese had apparently climbed unobserved, while the platoon had been engaged in beating back the first echelon. Another Machine Gun began firing from the right of the ridge, which hit Subedar Joginder Singh, who was moving between the two sections. Wounded on the thigh, the daring JCO dragged himself into the Platoon Headquarters Bunker, but continued the fire fight. His platoon accounted for most of the Chinese casualties. However, by now, most of the platoon had been either killed or seriously wounded, leaving just a handful of unhurt men.

The Chinese then moved some 200 men from the flank, and continued to clamber up. Subedar Joginder limped out of his bunker, and exhorted the living few, to be prepared to move out with fixed bayonets and charge on the enemy. Most of the enemy had been decimated, and the charge that followed, made sure that some 20 of those remaining were bayoneted and killed. At about this time, Lieutenant Hari Pal Kaushik, the Company Commander, came on the radio set and asked Subedar Joginder Singh if he could fire artillery and mortars onto the post (IB Ridge) itself. "Yes, Sahib" was the prompt reply. The artillery was then ordered to immediately engage the post with defensive fire 'Red-Over-Red', which meant, firing onto own positions with the likelihood of casualties to own troops. However, this would definitely take a heavier toll on the enemy, who were in the open. Three Other Ranks could however out mvre the enemy and get to the main defences at Sela.

Their actions recounted the resilience of the platoon, and the bravery of Subedar Joginder Singh in particular. This battle was fought for over Subedar Joginder Singh later succumbed to his injuries, and was posthumously awarded the Param Vir Chakra, for his gallant defence of Bum La. While the platoon at IB Ridge was fighting, the balance of Delta Company at Tongpen La had also beaten back a few attacks inflicting heavy casualties on the Chinese, who seemed to have broken contact and fallen back. However, in reality, the Chinese were planning to envelop the Company from the left and through the Geshe La Gorge, while simultaneously making deeper infiltration towards Tawang. Within an hour, a fresh enveloping attack developed on the two forward platoons. A concentration of about 700 Chinese was also observed in the nala (stream) below.

The company's MMGs and rifles laid concentrated fire on the enemy, who tried to rush through the platoons, by a wide hook around the Twin Peaks. However, the approach being narrow, the enemy could not push more than 150 men at a time along this route. Havildar Sarup Singh and his section, positioned there as a Protective Patrol, played havoc, resulting in heavy Chinese casualties. Utterly confused, the Chinese retreated, and the attack was temporarily beaten back. The enemy, however, re-mustered to make another determined attack on the left platoon, with the intention of rushing it by the sheer weight of numbers.

The troops were gallantly led by Lieutenant Hari Pal Kaushik, who moved from one section to another, encouraging his men to fight on with determination. He was awarded the Vir Chakra for his gallant actions. While the troops were fully entrenched and prepared to fight till the last, the Company was ordered to pull back as part of the overall plan, initially to the main defences at Milaktonga, which they did most successfully, and subsequently by 26 October 1962, with the Battalion, to Sela.
