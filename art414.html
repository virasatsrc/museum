Title: Defence of Poonch (1971)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

The Indo-Pakistan War of 1971 witnessed yet another attempt by the Pakistan Army to capture the strategically located border town of Poonch by employing its 12 Infantry Division. The capture would facilitate Pakistan's designs to expand their operations in Kashmir Valley, by giving them direct access to ingress routes over the passes on the Pir Panjal Ranges. 6 SIKH, under the command of Lieutenant Colonel KL Rattan, was holding defences over a 13 km frontage on the heights above Poonch. Point 405 and 406, two dominating features located North-East of the town, were held by the Battalion. These heights were key to the defence of Poonch, as they dominated the town as well as the Line of Control, giving a clear observation into the Kahuta Bowl, located deep inside Pakistan Occupied Kashmir. The Pakistanis were well aware that the success or failure of their plan to capture Poonch depended on their ability to capture these two features held by 6 SIKH - therefore since May 1971, the Battalion was in the process of improving its defences, reading battle indicators such as heavy enemy build-up opposite its defended localities.

The Pakistan plan was to launch two brigade size forces on Indian defences, North of Poonch - simultaneously, an Infantry battalion was to infiltrate towards East of Poonch. They were required to cut off the road, attack Poonch with another battalion and launch two Feint Attacks on Chechi Bandian, Gutrian and Shahpur. On the night of 03/04 December 1971, enemy Field and Medium Guns not only engaged the Battalion Defended Area, but also shelled the main town of Poonch and neighboring defences. After a heavy artillery barrage, at around 2200 hrs on 03 December 1971, a Multi-Directional Attack was launched on 6 SIKH localities at Helipad and Tund by 5 FRONTIER FORCE and 7 POK Brigade, respectively. Under cover of heavy artillery fire, a small group of enemy commandos successfully infiltrated near the unit's mortar position. However, the Mortar Platoon, with its impeccable organisation of their local protection under the command of Captain SS Dadhwal, beat back the skirmish.

The shortest and direct approach to Point 405 was held by two platoons under the command of Captain KS Mamik. By early morning of 04 December, numerous enemy attempts to progress operations along this approach had been foiled, including a day light attack. In a display of raw guts, Naik Naib Singh, an LMG Detachment Commander, realizing that he could not effectively engage the enemy from inside the bunker, came out into the communication trench and took a heavy toll on the enemy, before laying down his life. A patrol ahead of Point 405 observed the enemy advancing under the cover of the heavy artillery fire. Fore warned by the patrol, Bravo Company under Major Punjab Singh engaged the enemy effectively by mortar and artillery fire, which played havoc on the attackers. The enemy's 7 POK Brigade suffered heavy losses and had no option but to withdraw, leaving behind a large number of men killed, mule loads of ammunition and large quantities of warlike stores.

Such was the importance of the positions held by 6 SIKH, that in addition to simultaneous infantry attacks, the enemy also infiltrated a team of SSG Commandos from the rear, in order to make headway towards Point 405. Around noon of 04 December, enemy troops opened another approach, and reached within 30-40 metres of the Point 405. This enemy attack was led by a Rocket Launcher team under Lance Naik Noor Shahjahan. This young soldier was killed and was later awarded the 'Sitara-e-Jurat" (posthumous), based on the description of his brave act given by 6 SIKH after the war. This was an act of honouring a brave deed even though it was that of an enemy soldier.

Throughout 04 and 05 December, the enemy could not make any headway. 6 SIKH foiled repeated attempts of the enemy, displaying outstanding valour. Eventually, with no success to boast of and heavy casualties, the enemy withdrew. Under the resolute leadership of Major KS Padda, the situation was exploited, and by first light on 07 December, the operational situation was fully under the Battalion's control. Various localities had their own share of gallant deeds and sacrifices. Lance Naik Lal Singh, one of the best boxers in the unit, evacuated Sepoy Charan Singh under intense enemy fire and artillery shelling in true tradition of the regiment. The artillery observation post located at Point 406A also made significant contribution by taking effective shots - it successfully interdicted the enemy reinforcements on the Mohammad-ki-Gali-Kahuta Road.

The Battalion was awarded the Battle Honour - 'Defence of Poonch' and Theatre Honour - Jammu and Kashmir-1971'. A total of eight gallantry awards were earned by the unit in this battle, including the Maha Vir Chakra to Lieutenant Colonel (later Major General) KL Rattan, five Vir Chakras to Major Panjab Singh, Havildar Malkiat Singh, Sepoy Sampuran Singh, Naik Naib Singh and Sepoy (Housekeeper) Mangat Ram, who without any fear or adversity, continuously supplied ammunition to own position under attack till his last breath - the latter two were posthumous. 6 SIKH also had two Mention-in-Dispatches for outstanding performance in battle.
