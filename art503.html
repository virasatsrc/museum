Machine Gun Companies and Battalion

Title: Machine Gun Companies and Battalion
Category: Beyond Battle Fd
Artist: Sikh Regimental Centre
Year: Historical

During the Army's Reorganisation post the First World War, a need was felt to enhance the fire power of a battalion. Therefore, the Delta Company of each unit was converted into a Machine Gun (MG) Company which had six Machine Guns manned by 87 men who were a mix of Sikhs and Punjabi Mussalmans. The gun was a Point .303 inch Vickers Mk I and the men were armed with .303 inch Short Magazine Lee-Enfield Rifles.

Later on, at the end of 1941, the General Headquarters India decided to raise a MG Battalion for each infantry group. The MG Battalion of SIKH Regiment, 11 SIKH REGIMENT was raised on 15 January 1942 by Lieutenant Colonel JCB Church who had prewar experience on Vickers Machine Guns. The nucleus of this new raising consisted of manpower drawn from MG Companies of various battalions of the Regiment.

The Battalion had four MG Companies, each comprising of 200 All Ranks. Alpha and Bravo Companies were Sikhs, while Charlie and Delta Companies comprised of Punjabi Mussalmans. The Battalion was equipped with 48 MGs, 102 Wheeled and eight Tracked Vehicles.

After raising, the Battalion underwent Jungle Warfare training at Chhindwara in 1943 and then moved to Ranchi where it was equipped with Medium Machine Guns (MMGs). The Battalion joined 19 Infantry Division at Nasik in July 1944. It moved to Imphal on 01 November 1944 and served in the allied campaign against the Japanese in Burma. During operations, the MG Battalion was distributed with 64 and 98 Indian Infantry Brigades.

The Battalion crossed the Chindwin River on 16 December 1944. Battalion Headquarters and two Punjabi-Mussalman Companies were involved in the Battle of Kabwet on the Irrawaddy River from 11 January to 02 February 1945. At Mandalay, 36 Guns of the Battalion deployed massive firepower, supporting 19 Division through Meiktila, Thazi, and along the Mawchi Road. The Battalion sailed for Calcutta in April 1946 and relocated to Nowshera. On 10 November 1946, the Battalion was disbanded after proving its mettle and devotion of the highest order.
