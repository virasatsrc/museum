Title: Battle of Walong (1962)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

On 26 September 1962, 4 SIKH less two rifle companies was airlifted to Walong just before the outbreak of the Sino-Indian hostilities. A day later, Alpha and Bravo Companies occupied initial defences on the East and West Banks of Lohit River, at heights varying from 5000 to 8000 feet.The long lines of communication accentuated the logistic problems faced by troops deployed astride a major river obstacle. On the night of 20/21 October 1962, the Chinese launched a major offensive across the McMahon Line in the Kibithu Sector. The balance two rifle companies of 4 SIKH were airlifted to Walong by 23 October, as the Chinese offensive was progressing.

While the battle was being fought ahead by 6 KUMAON, between the McMahon Line and Walong, 4 SIKH re-adjusted its defences and was hastily deployed in areas straddling the Lohit River, as under:-

(a) On the East Bank, Charlie Company at Dong Plateau and Alpha Company at Dong Hill.

(b) On the West Bank, Delta Company at Lachman Ridge and Bravo Company at Maha Plateau (including Mithun Track and Ladders).

The Chinese drove in the forward positions, occupied by elements of 6 KUMAON and ASSAM RIFLES troops, and came into contact with 4 SIKH defences at Walong. The Chinese, some 600 strong, collided with a platoon of Bravo Company, at Ladders. On the morning of 24 October, the Chinese were given a bloody nose and they dispersed. Over 200 Chinese lay dead or wounded, and the rest fled. The Chinese re-organised themselves, and a second attack came in by the evening. Once again, the Sikh gallantry was demonstrated prominently. The enemy had set the dry grass on fire in the entire post and tried to achieve surprise by moving close behind the spreading fire, but were promptly beaten back. The enemy again assaulted Bravo Company on the following night (26/27 October). It was here that the most gallant acts of bravery were witnessed, one such exemplary action was that of 20-year-old Sepoy Kewal Singh, who had barely 18 months of service.

On the night of 26 October 1962, when the Chinese threatened his Company's position, Sepoy Kewal Singh, on his own initiative, rushed out of his section post and charged the enemy with his bayonet, killing a few Chinese in hand-to-hand fighting. He was seriously wounded in this encounter. Despite grievous injuries, he bayoneted another enemy soldier before succumbing to his injuries. For this act of valour and self-sacrifice, Sepoy Kewal Singh was awarded the Maha Vir Chakra. Remaining in contact with the enemy and obtaining information of his moves was important. To this effect, patrols moved out from all the companies from both the flanks of the Lohit River to dominate the 'No Man's Land'. A Patrol Base was established North-West of Maha Plateau, from where extensive patrolling could be carried out towards Yellow Pimple.

The Chinese kept spilling forward, not only towards the Patrol Base, but towards tri-junction, and the Northern part of Yepak Ridge. Sporadic Chinese artillery shelling also continued from the temporary position, which was suitably replied by Indian artillery and mortars. By the morning of 15 November, there were strong indications of a major Chinese attack from the Western and Eastern flanks. The Chinese repeatedly tried to dislodge the troops from the Patrol Base, but despite heavy casualties, the men held on to their positions, fighting from behind boulders and trees. Ammunition was however running low, and enemy pressure increasing. Ammunition had to be carried to the forward companies by all the Non-Combatants and Tradesmen.

The situation became grim by 15 November 1962. By about 2030 hrs, enemy firing and shelling became intense. Wave after wave, the enemy tried to force himself onto the Maha Plateau position, but the men of 4 SIKH held on. Followed by intense fighting, the attack was repulsed with heavy losses to the enemy. Casualties in the Indian side were also heavy. The Alpha Company Commander, Major PM Samvatsar got injured due to shelling, refused to be evacuated and died on the battlefield; all the Platoon Commanders were also wounded. The conduct of Alpha Company was marked with bravery, for they had performed splendidly, and with drew only when ordered. Lieutenant Yog Raj Palta (fondly called 'Joe') gave a formidable account of his bravery at High Plateau locality. While beating back an enemy attack, he received a burst of fire and was killed - his dying words to his men were, "Eat the enemy raw, do not leave them". He was awarded a Vir Chakra posthumously. The enemy launched simultaneous attacks on High Plateau, Mithun Track and Maha Plateau which were repulsed by artillery fire.

Subsequently, some additional fire support was provided, but it was not enough to sustain all the positions against the heavy Chinese onslaught. 4 SIKH was running out of ammunition and a re-supply was urgently required. Lance Havildar Kirpa Ram, the unit's boot maker volunteered and persuaded other Tradesmen and Non-Combatants to take up the task of re-supplying ammunition to forward troops. He set a shining example of bravery on 06 November, when the situation was very grave at Maha Plateau. Kirpa gallantly fought his way through the enemy who had blocked the route, but was seriously wounded. Though incapacitated, he kept on cheering his comrades to fulfill their pledged mission, ensuring that ammunition was delivered, which enabled the Company to fight for a longer period. For this act of valour, he was awarded a Vir Chakra, posthumously.

During the 25 days of battle at Walong, 4 SIKH had distinguished itself and won a number of gallantry awards - but suffered 82 killed, 97 wounded and 20 taken as PoW, making precious sacrifices in service of the country.
