Title: Neuve Chapelle & La Bassee (1914)
Category: Battle Gallery
Regiment: Sikh Regiment

The Indian Expeditionary Force (Le.. The 3rd (Lahore) Division), comprising the 7th (Ferozepore). 8th (Jullundur) and 9th (Sirhind) Brigades, were diverted to France, where the British Expeditionary Force was shattered and exhausted after two months of bitter fighting against the German Army's overwhelming Numbers. At 1700 hrs, On 27 October 1914, the Germans broke through the South of Neuve Chapelle. The danger of a gap being created between the defensive positions of the 3rd and the 5th Division was large. A breakthrough by the Germans could have seriously affected the defensive positions in the area.

The enemy resistance in the hamlet South of Neuve Chapelle was extremely tough, and during the stiff and heavy fighting. many casualties were suffered. Immediately after this action, there followed the Battle of La Bassee on 28 October 1914. This battle was to transform XLVII SIKHS into storm troopers. Two Companies of XLVII SIKHS, plus Sappers and Miners, under Major SR Davidson, had moved into line. but considerable gaps remained. Frequent attacks were made throughout the night by the Germans, but these were repulsed with Heavy Machine Gun fire. The searchlights at Neuve Chapelle had swept the front and the trenches all night. Its capture was a preliminary necessity for the Allies.

The fall of Neuve Chapelle would facilitate the capture of Lille. From there, the Allies would be in a fair position to move against the Germans, between that point and the sea.

The Germans were strongly placed in shattered houses on the fringe of Bois-du-Biez. The British line now ran in a curved fashion, around the village, with the Germans closeted at a distance of about 100 yards. The position was held from right to left by 21st Company Sappers and Miners, two companies of XLVII SIKHS, 20th Company Sappers and Miners, the 4th ROYAL FUSILIERS and the 1st LINCOLNSHIRE. The Operational Orders for 28 October 1914 were issued by General Smith-Dorrien, at 2300 hrs on 27 October. It was directed that whilst other troops remained on the defensive, the 3rd Division should counter-attack the Germans of Neuve Chapelle. The attack on Neuve Chapelle was finally carried out by only four companies, two of XLVII SIKHS, together with the 20th and the 21st Company Sappers and Miners on either side of them, and with the greatest of gallantry.

The right flank was to be protected by the 9th BHOPALS, but as the unit advanced, it came under very heavy fire and halted. It only continued to fire from a trench line they had reached The Sikhs forged on, covering the seven hundred yards of open ground between them and Neuve Chapelle by rushes. alternating with fire, as if on a trg ground. Soon, the four companies reached the ruins of the village. Casualties were numerous, but their excellent fire control saved much heavier losses. The Indians were about 100 yards from the outskirts of the village, when the Germans in the front trenches began to bolt. The Germans were pursued by the gallant Sikhs and the Sappers with the bayonets. They moved along the streets in small groups, in complete synchronisation. After desperate hand-to-hand fighting, by degrees, the houses were cleared. In this endeavour, One Sepoy of XLVII SIKHS is reported to have single-handedly captured three Germans out of eight, having previously killed the other five.

From another house, the Sikhs recovered a wounded British soldier and two wounded Germans. On reaching the cross roads in the centre of the village, the troops come under severe Machine Gun fire. Captain McCleverty, always in advance, applauded his men. He dashed across the roads, the rest following close on his heels. But he was shot dead at the comer, by a German concealed only a few yards away, Losses were rapidly becoming serious, due to the enemy rifle fire from the houses. Several Machine Guns rated the Eastern and Northern outskirts, where they were met by heavy shell and Machine Gun fire, Counter-attack after counter-attack were launched against the Sikhs. The Germans used the bodies of their own dead as cover. Major SR Davidson of XLVII SIKHS was collecting his men for a final charge, when the German came in overpowering numbers from the North and East. At the same moment, the Machine Gunfire re-doubled its fury down the main street.

Without immediate reinforcements, the position of XLVII SIKHS was quite untenable, as their losses had been very heavy, Major Davidson was mus compelled to give up all he had won of such fearful cost and retire. Eventually, the remains of the two companies of XLVII SIKHS gof back to comparative safely, but only 68 out of the gallant 289 were actually collected on the La Bassee Road. The official war history record states The attack was magnificently carried out, and was within an ace of success. It is probable that, had reinforcements been available. XLVII SIKHS and Sappers would have held the village which they took with such superb elan and of such a heavy cost.
